# ADR-034 --- Strategy, StrategyMatch and Trading Opportunity Boundaries

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-21

## Context

Trading OS has validated its runtime foundation through Story 0008 and a
successful authenticated ActiveScan benchmark:

``` text
Gateway
    → ActiveScan
    → Market Data acquisition
    → AnalysisExecution
    → Capability execution
    → PipelineRun
    → Observation
    → TradingOpportunity
    → ActiveScan result projection
```

The benchmark exposed a domain-design question rather than an infrastructure
defect.

A scan over the current broad market universe produced approximately 1337
`TradingOpportunity` instances from roughly 1379 successful eligible analyses.

Source inspection confirms why.

The current opportunity pipeline is:

``` text
AnalysisExecution
    → capabilities
    → ConsolidatedIntelligence
    → ObservationBuilder
    → OhlcTrendObservationRule
    → OpportunityEngine
    → TradingOpportunity
```

`OhlcTrendObservationRule` currently behaves as an implicit trading strategy:

-   entry condition: OHLC price change is not zero;
-   direction: sign of the first-to-last price change (LONG/SHORT);
-   timeframe: hardcoded `15m`;
-   scenario: hardcoded `OHLC_TREND`;
-   validity window: observation time plus 30 minutes.

This behavior is deterministic and explainable, which is consistent with the
existing architecture. It was acceptable as an implementation foundation.

It is nevertheless insufficient for the intended Quant Trading model because:

-   there is no explicit Strategy identity or version;
-   there is no strategy lifecycle or validation state;
-   there is no research provenance;
-   the effective entry condition (`priceChange != 0`) is nearly always true
    on liquid markets over a trending window, which explains the very high
    opportunity rate;
-   opportunity semantics are owned implicitly by pipeline code inside Market
    Intelligence instead of by an explicit domain concept;
-   live evaluation semantics cannot be reused by a future Backtest without
    duplicating rules, creating semantic-drift risk.

The long-term Quant architecture separates four concerns:

1.  Quant Research --- discovers and studies potential edges/hypotheses.
2.  Strategy --- formalizes a hypothesis into deterministic setup rules.
3.  Backtest --- evaluates Strategy behavior on historical data.
4.  Scanner --- applies enabled Strategies to current market state.

This ADR defines the domain boundaries between Observation, Strategy,
StrategyMatch, TradingOpportunity, Scanner, Backtest and Quant Research before
Story 0009 begins.

## Decision Question

> Who owns the definition of what constitutes a trading opportunity, and how
> should Strategy, StrategyMatch, Observation and TradingOpportunity relate to
> each other in Trading OS?

Secondary questions:

-   Should Strategy be a first-class domain concept?
-   Should StrategyMatch exist explicitly?
-   Should StrategyMatch be persisted?
-   Should TradingOpportunity derive from StrategyMatch?
-   Should live Scanner and Backtest share deterministic Strategy evaluation
    semantics?
-   Where should Strategy initially live?
-   How does this preserve existing Scanner, Risk and Broker boundaries?

## Domain Definitions

The following definitions are authoritative for Trading OS.

### Observation

An Observation is a deterministic, explainable statement about current or
historical market state, derived from intelligence evidence (capability results
and artifacts) and independent of any trading strategy.

Observations remain upstream knowledge. They are reusable by many strategies.
They do not imply an opportunity.

### StrategyDefinition

A StrategyDefinition is a versioned definition of the deterministic conditions
under which a trading setup is considered valid for a given market universe,
timeframe and direction.

### StrategyEvaluation

A StrategyEvaluation is one execution of a StrategyDefinition against a
specific market context; it may match or not match.

### StrategyMatch

A StrategyMatch is an immutable fact that a specific Strategy version satisfied
its conditions for a specific market and context at a specific time.

### TradingOpportunity

A TradingOpportunity is a trader-facing actionable candidate derived from a
successful StrategyMatch.

### TradePlan

A TradePlan is a concrete proposal for entry, stop, target, size and execution
parameters derived from an opportunity.

### RiskEvaluation

A RiskEvaluation is the deterministic, authoritative verdict of the Risk Domain
on a TradePlan.

### ExecutionIntent

An ExecutionIntent is a human-authorized instruction to attempt broker
execution of a validated trade plan.

## Current State Assessment

The following elements are confirmed by source inspection.

### What works well

-   Observations are deterministic, explainable, traceable, versioned and
    lineage-aware (ADR-025).
-   The pipeline produces truthful no-signal outcomes when evidence is absent.
-   Provenance from capability executions to observations to opportunities
    already exists.
-   Scanner orchestration (Stories 0006/0007) is durable, idempotent and
    projects results truthfully.
-   Story 0008 guarantees fresh market snapshots without manual subscription.

### What is insufficient

-   `OhlcTrendObservationRule` embeds trading-setup semantics inside Market
    Intelligence pipeline code with no strategy identity.
-   The direct path from generic Observation to TradingOpportunity promotes
    weak signals into trader-facing candidates.
-   Direction, timeframe, scenario and validity are hardcoded in pipeline
    logic rather than defined by a versioned rule set.
-   No persisted artifact answers "which strategy produced this opportunity?"

This is an architectural gap, not an implementation bug. The current system
truthfully reports what its implicit single pseudo-strategy detects; the
problem is that the pseudo-strategy is nearly vacuous and anonymous.

## Evaluated Alternatives

### Option A --- Keep the current Observation-driven Opportunity model

Observation directly implies Opportunity; Strategy remains implicit.

Positive:

-   zero implementation cost;
-   full compatibility with current behavior.

Negative:

-   strategy semantics cannot be versioned or governed;
-   backtest/live divergence risk when rules evolve;
-   weak provenance (no strategy identity on opportunities);
-   trader-learning analytics impossible without strategy attribution;
-   opportunity volume remains unbounded and semantically empty.

Rejected as the target model.

### Option B --- Strategy as configuration inside Market Intelligence

Introduce strategy configuration consumed by a rule engine, without a
first-class Strategy domain or persisted StrategyMatch.

Positive:

-   smaller implementation cost than Option C;
-   centralizes rule definitions.

Negative:

-   lifecycle/status/validation states have no domain owner;
-   configuration alone cannot carry research provenance or backtest linkage;
-   matches remain transient, weakening auditability and analytics;
-   still requires later migration to a real domain.

Rejected: it defers the ownership question without answering it.

### Option C --- First-class Strategy domain plus persisted StrategyMatch

Introduce `StrategyDefinition`, a deterministic evaluator, persisted immutable
`StrategyMatch`, and derive `TradingOpportunity` from a match.

Positive:

-   explicit ownership of setup semantics;
-   versioning enables safe evolution and comparison;
-   auditability and reproducibility through persisted matches;
-   one evaluator serves both live scanning and future backtesting;
-   strategy-attributed provenance enables Trader Analytics;
-   incremental, non-breaking adoption is possible.

Negative:

-   new domain complexity and persistence;
-   version management and governance overhead;
-   migration work to port the current pseudo-strategy.

Selected.

### Option D --- Separate Strategy Engine microservice

Strategy lives in its own service performing remote evaluation.

Positive:

-   strong isolation;
-   independent scalability.

Negative:

-   premature service split for a personal trading platform;
-   network latency and consistency cost inside the per-market analysis
    budget;
-   duplicates Market Intelligence context assembly;
-   operational complexity without current need.

Rejected for now; extraction remains possible later.

## Decision

Trading OS SHALL introduce Strategy as a first-class domain concept and SHALL
make TradingOpportunity derive from a persisted StrategyMatch.

### Strategy Responsibility

A StrategyDefinition owns:

-   stable strategy identity;
-   strategy version;
-   lifecycle status;
-   applicable market universe and asset class;
-   applicable timeframes;
-   required observations/features;
-   deterministic entry conditions;
-   direction rules;
-   invalidation conditions;
-   regime/liquidity/spread constraints where relevant;
-   parameter set;
-   provenance from research;
-   backtest validation reference/status.

The exact physical schema is an implementation concern for Story 0009.

### Strategy Non-Responsibilities

Strategy MUST NOT own:

-   account or risk authorization;
-   position sizing authority;
-   broker execution;
-   Market Data acquisition;
-   Scanner orchestration;
-   AI interpretation authority;
-   final human execution authorization;
-   cross-market ranking calibration.

### Strategy Lifecycle

The following lifecycle applies at architectural level:

``` text
DRAFT → CANDIDATE → VALIDATED → ENABLED → RETIRED
```

Semantics:

-   DRAFT: under construction; never evaluated in production scans.
-   CANDIDATE: proposed for validation; may be evaluated in experiments only.
-   VALIDATED: backed by accepted validation evidence (initially backtest
    evidence once Backtest exists).
-   ENABLED: eligible for live Scanner evaluation.
-   RETIRED: no longer eligible; historical matches remain valid facts.

Human approval boundaries:

-   CANDIDATE → VALIDATED requires explicit human approval of the supporting
    evidence.
-   VALIDATED → ENABLED requires explicit human approval.

AI MAY propose hypotheses, summarize results, suggest parameters and explain
failures. AI MUST NOT silently promote a Strategy across lifecycle transitions
and MUST NOT alter deterministic evaluation results.

### Bootstrap / Legacy Strategy Exception

The existing `OhlcTrendObservationRule` will be ported into an explicit
strategy purely as a migration vehicle. This bootstrap/legacy strategy exists
only to:

-   migrate current semantics into the Strategy architecture;
-   prove StrategyEvaluator integration;
-   prove StrategyMatch persistence;
-   prove TradingOpportunity derivation and lineage;
-   preserve current observable behavior during refactoring.

Its current condition (`priceChange != 0`) is intentionally weak and nearly
vacuous. Its existence MUST NOT be interpreted as quantitative evidence of
trading edge, and it MUST NOT be silently labeled as quantitatively validated.

To express this truthfully without adding lifecycle states, validation is
modeled as explicit validation metadata on the StrategyDefinition rather than
as an extra lifecycle status:

``` text
validationStatus = UNVALIDATED | VALIDATED
```

Semantics:

-   the lifecycle position (DRAFT..ENABLED) expresses governance intent;
-   `validationStatus` expresses whether accepted deterministic evidence
    (initially Backtest evidence) actually supports the strategy.

A strategy without accepted validation evidence carries
`validationStatus = UNVALIDATED` regardless of its lifecycle position. During
the migration phase (before Backtest exists), the bootstrap legacy strategy is
permitted to run under controlled evaluation so that Stories 0009–0012 can be
proven; this is an explicitly temporary, human-approved exception, not a
precedent for enabling unvalidated strategies in general. Once Backtest and
the validation lifecycle exist (Stories 0013–0014), ENABLED strategies are
expected to carry `validationStatus = VALIDATED`.

This was chosen over adding a BOOTSTRAP/LEGACY lifecycle state because it
keeps the lifecycle minimal while making validation truthfulness explicit and
queryable.

### StrategyMatch Decision

StrategyMatch SHALL exist explicitly as a persisted immutable domain fact, not
as a transient result and not as an embedded field of TradingOpportunity.

Persistence is justified because matches must support:

-   audit;
-   replay;
-   reproducibility;
-   versioned strategy performance analysis;
-   live versus backtest comparison;
-   future Trader Analytics;
-   AI explanations grounded in recorded facts;
-   debugging.

Minimum conceptual provenance of a StrategyMatch:

-   strategyId;
-   strategyVersion;
-   marketId;
-   analysisExecutionId;
-   matchedAt;
-   observation/artifact references;
-   condition results;
-   context digest.

Physical schema is deferred to implementation.

### TradingOpportunity Decision

For V1, one TradingOpportunity SHALL derive from exactly one StrategyMatch.

Multiple-strategy confluence MAY be introduced later as an explicit extension;
it must not be smuggled in implicitly.

Existing TradingOpportunity fields remain relevant:

-   instrument, direction, timeframe, validity window, score, explanation and
    lifecycle stay trader-facing attributes;

but their semantics SHALL eventually originate from the StrategyDefinition and
the StrategyMatch instead of hardcoded pipeline values.

### Scanner Boundary

This ADR extends ADR-033; it does not redesign it.

The Scanner continues to own:

-   user intent;
-   account-aware scope resolution;
-   market universe resolution;
-   durable orchestration;
-   dispatch, progress and result projection.

The Scanner MUST NOT define trading setup semantics.

Future conceptual chain:

``` text
ActiveScanIntent
    ↓
scope resolution
    ↓
applicable Strategy selection
    ↓
market intelligence
    ↓
Strategy evaluation
    ↓
StrategyMatch
    ↓
TradingOpportunity
```

Strategy selection SHOULD be implemented as a Strategy application service
consumed by the Scanner, keeping orchestration and applicability decisions
separate. ActiveScan persistence is unchanged by this ADR.

### Backtest Contract

The following invariant is established:

> The same deterministic StrategyDefinition and StrategyEvaluator semantics
> used for live scanning MUST be reusable for historical Backtest evaluation.

Conceptual model:

``` text
StrategyDefinition
       ↓
StrategyEvaluator
      / \
     /   \
LiveContext  HistoricalContext
   ↓              ↓
Scanner        Backtest
```

Implementing separate live and backtest rule sets for the same strategy is
forbidden, because duplicated semantics inevitably drift. The
StrategyEvaluator is the shared deterministic authority: Backtest must not
reimplement strategy conditions, and Scanner must not reimplement strategy
conditions.

This invariant does NOT require live and historical context to share identical
physical DTOs. It requires that both expose the same strategy-required
semantic inputs. A future abstraction, provisionally named
`StrategyEvaluationContext`, is the recommended boundary: it defines the
semantic inputs a StrategyDefinition may consume (prices, observations,
features, snapshot state), with live and historical providers each producing
it. This ADR recommends the abstraction; it does not implement it. This ADR
defines the contract only; the Backtest engine itself remains out of scope.

### Semantic Distinction of "Opportunity"

The following distinction is fundamental and must remain unambiguous:

-   Observation --- "Something is happening in the market."
-   StrategyMatch --- "This market currently satisfies Strategy X version Y."
-   TradingOpportunity --- "This StrategyMatch is exposed to the trader as an
    actionable candidate."
-   TradePlan --- "Here is a concrete proposed trade."
-   RiskEvaluation --- "This proposed trade is / is not allowed."

The Story 0008 benchmark (~1337 opportunities from ~1379 successful analyses)
demonstrated concretely why Observation must not directly imply Opportunity.

### Quant Research Boundary

Quant Research owns:

-   formulating hypotheses;
-   exploring datasets and features;
-   proposing candidate strategies;
-   interpreting experiments;
-   generating validation evidence.

Quant Research does NOT automatically enable production strategies. The path
from ResearchHypothesis to Enabled Strategy passes through explicit human
validation at the CANDIDATE→VALIDATED and VALIDATED→ENABLED transitions.

AI assists research and interpretation; it never falsifies metrics, overrides
deterministic evaluation, or silently enables strategies.

### Trader Analytics Compatibility

Strategy identity/version and match lineage MUST be preserved through:

``` text
StrategyDefinition/version
    → StrategyEvaluation
    → StrategyMatch
    → TradingOpportunity
    → TradePlan
    → RiskEvaluation
    → ExecutionIntent
    → Trade
    → TradeOutcome
```

Preserving this lineage now keeps future behavioral questions answerable
without redesigning historical data, including:

-   which strategies the trader executes best;
-   which valid matches the trader ignores;
-   which strategies the trader systematically overtrades;
-   the delay between a StrategyMatch and actual entry;
-   whether discretionary intervention improves or reduces expectancy;
-   whether live performance diverges from backtest expectations;
-   performance per market regime.

Implementing these links and analytics is future work; today's architecture
must simply not destroy the required provenance.

### Naming Collision

The repository contains `ActiveAnalysisStrategy` and `PassiveAnalysisStrategy`.
These are orchestration/capability-selection profiles, not trading Strategies.

Future terminology SHOULD rename them toward `ActiveAnalysisProfile` /
`PassiveAnalysisProfile`. This ADR does not perform the rename; until then,
the term "Strategy" in documentation refers exclusively to the trading
Strategy domain defined here.

### Score and Ranking Boundary

This ADR does NOT move all scoring into Strategy.

-   Match confidence belongs to StrategyEvaluation output.
-   Opportunity priority/ranking remains a separate concern.
-   Cross-market ranking remains unresolved, exactly as stated in ADR-033:
    raw OpportunityScore MUST NOT be interpreted as globally calibrated
    ranking.

### Module Placement

Strategy SHALL initially live inside the Market Intelligence service,
because it consumes intelligence context and produces matches adjacent to
the existing pipeline, while avoiding a premature microservice split.

The physical deployment boundary is not the domain ownership boundary.
Strategy SHALL be implemented as an isolated bounded module inside the
market-intelligence codebase, conceptually structured as:

``` text
market-intelligence
│
├── intelligence
│   ├── observations
│   ├── capabilities
│   ├── context
│   └── pipeline
│
└── strategy
    ├── domain
    │   ├── StrategyDefinition
    │   ├── StrategyLifecycle
    │   ├── StrategyMatch
    │   └── ...
    │
    ├── application
    │   ├── StrategyEvaluator
    │   ├── StrategySelectionService
    │   └── ...
    │
    └── persistence/adapters
```

This structure is conceptual architecture, not an implementation instruction
for this ADR. Its purpose is to prevent Strategy from degrading into "another
Market Intelligence pipeline rule": it is its own domain boundary, colocated
inside the same deployable service. The intelligence pipeline may consume the
Strategy module through its application services; it must not reach into
Strategy internals, and Strategy must not depend on pipeline internals.

Extraction into a dedicated Quant Research/Strategy service becomes justified
only when research workflows, backtesting scale, or team boundaries demand it.

## Compatibility With Existing Work

-   ADR-025 (Observation Model): UNCHANGED. Observations remain
    strategy-independent upstream knowledge.
-   ADR-026 (Trading Opportunity Model): EXTENDED. Opportunities gain a
    mandatory derivation from StrategyMatch; existing fields and lifecycle
    remain.
-   ADR-033 (Active/Passive Scanner): EXTENDED. Scanner gains optional
    strategy targeting; orchestration semantics unchanged.
-   Story 0006 (durable ActiveScan): UNCHANGED.
-   Story 0007 (lifecycle/result projection): UNCHANGED; projections later
    surface strategy attribution.
-   Story 0008 (snapshot acquisition): UNCHANGED.
-   Risk Domain: UNCHANGED. Strategy never authorizes risk.
-   Broker Service: UNCHANGED. Strategy never touches execution.

No conflicts were identified.

## Consequences

### Positive

-   Explicit ownership of edge/setup semantics.
-   Versioned, governable strategy evolution.
-   Auditability and reproducibility of every opportunity.
-   Truthful meaning for "opportunity".
-   One evaluator prevents backtest/live drift.
-   Strategy-attributed lineage enables Trader Analytics.
-   Clean integration point for future Quant Research.

### Negative

-   New domain complexity and persistence.
-   Version management and lifecycle governance.
-   Additive migrations and evolution work.
-   Evaluator performance considerations at large market-universe scale.
-   Migration of the current pseudo-strategy into an explicit legacy strategy.

## Migration Strategy

Non-breaking, incremental order:

1.  Introduce StrategyDefinition domain semantics as a bounded module.
2.  Introduce a deterministic StrategyEvaluator behind a port.
3.  Port the current OHLC trend behavior as an explicit bootstrap legacy
    strategy (behavior-preserving), marked `validationStatus = UNVALIDATED`,
    so that architectural refactoring is provable separately from
    strategy-quality improvement.
4.  Introduce persisted StrategyMatch with provenance.
5.  Make TradingOpportunity derive from StrategyMatch.
6.  Introduce Backtest reusing the same evaluator.
7.  Introduce the Strategy validation lifecycle on top of Backtest evidence.
8.  Extend ActiveScan to select applicable strategies.
9.  Introduce the Quant Research workflow.

Steps 1–5 preserve observable behavior except that opportunities become
strategy-attributed; tightening the entry conditions is deliberately deferred
so that semantic changes are separate, reviewable decisions.

## Recommended Implementation Sequence

The lifecycle's validation evidence depends on Backtest. A production Scanner
therefore MUST NOT require genuinely validated ENABLED strategies before a
validation mechanism exists. The sequence below resolves this ordering:
the bootstrap legacy strategy runs under the temporary, human-approved
exception described above until validation infrastructure exists.

-   Story 0009 --- Strategy Domain Foundation.
-   Story 0010 --- Deterministic StrategyEvaluator.
-   Story 0011 --- StrategyMatch Persistence and Provenance.
-   Story 0012 --- TradingOpportunity from StrategyMatch.
-   Story 0013 --- Backtest Foundation.
-   Story 0014 --- Strategy Validation Lifecycle.
-   Story 0015 --- Strategy-Targeted Active Scanner.

Then, in order:

-   Quant Research Foundation.
-   Trader Analytics, once sufficient live trade/outcome lineage exists.

Story 0009 scope: introduce the Strategy bounded module inside market-
intelligence with `StrategyDefinition`, strategy identity/version,
lifecycle status, `validationStatus` metadata, applicability attributes and
persistence, without wiring it into the production pipeline or scanner.

## Rejected Alternatives

### Alternative 1 --- Keep Observation-driven Opportunities indefinitely

Rejected: it leaves opportunity semantics anonymous, unversioned and
unattributable, blocking backtesting and analytics.

### Alternative 2 --- Strategy as pure configuration

Rejected: configuration cannot own lifecycle, provenance or validation state.

### Alternative 3 --- Separate Strategy microservice now

Rejected: premature split; duplication of context assembly; operational cost.

### Alternative 4 --- Multiple strategies per opportunity in V1

Rejected: confluence introduces ranking/fusion semantics before the base
model exists; deferred as an explicit future extension.

### Alternative 5 --- Let AI decide whether conditions constitute a match

Rejected: AI must not be the authority for deterministic strategy conditions.

### Alternative 6 --- Add a BOOTSTRAP/LEGACY lifecycle state

Rejected: it overloads lifecycle governance with migration concerns. A
separate `validationStatus` metadata field expresses validation truthfulness
without complicating the lifecycle.

## Non-Negotiable Invariants

1.  Strategy owns the definition of what constitutes a valid setup.
2.  Scanner owns how the search is orchestrated, never what a setup is.
3.  StrategyMatch is a persisted immutable fact.
4.  Every V1 TradingOpportunity derives from exactly one StrategyMatch.
5.  Observations remain strategy-independent upstream knowledge.
6.  Live scanning and Backtest share one deterministic evaluator.
7.  CANDIDATE→VALIDATED and VALIDATED→ENABLED require human approval.
8.  AI never silently promotes strategies or alters evaluation results.
9.  Strategy never owns risk authorization, sizing, execution or ranking
    calibration.
10. Strategy initially lives within Market Intelligence as an isolated
    bounded module, not as pipeline logic.
11. The bootstrap legacy strategy is a migration vehicle with
    `validationStatus = UNVALIDATED`; it never counts as validation evidence.
12. Physical deployment boundary does not collapse domain ownership
    boundaries: the intelligence pipeline consumes Strategy through its
    application services only.

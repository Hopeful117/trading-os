# ADR-045 - Broker Facts and Mode-Specific Execution Boundaries

## Status

Proposed - Human approval required

## Date

2026-09-19

## Context

The PAPER journey requires broker capability and margin facts while preserving
the existing architecture:

* Market Data owns instrument metadata and market constraints.
* Trading Core owns user workflows, financial context assembly, execution
  lifecycle, and PAPER financial state.
* Risk Domain owns deterministic authorization.
* Broker Service isolates provider communication and reports technical facts.
* LIVE positions are broker-authoritative.
* PAPER positions are Trading Core-authoritative.

The implementation must not solve the PAPER risk gap by moving position
authority or business decisions into Broker Service.

## Decision

This ADR clarifies the implementation boundary for Stories 0050, 0051, and
0052. It does not amend ADR-006, ADR-028, ADR-029, ADR-030, ADR-042, or ADR-044.

### 1. Market constraints

Market Data remains authoritative for instrument metadata and market constraints,
including price precision, quantity precision, minimum order size, and minimum
cost.

Broker Service may report additional provider/account technical capabilities,
but must not duplicate or silently override Market Data ownership.

### 2. Broker capabilities

Broker Service exposes immutable broker-neutral technical facts through internal
contracts. Examples include:

* supported provider/account leverage levels;
* provider-supported order capabilities;
* technical margin facts for a concrete request;
* source version and observation timestamp.

Provider-specific payloads, authentication, mapping, and error semantics remain
inside Broker Service.

### 3. Risk authority

Trading Core assembles the immutable Risk Evaluation Context. The Risk Domain
decides whether the context is approved, rejected, or unavailable.

Broker Service never authorizes a trade and never changes risk rules.

### 4. Execution authority by mode

Execution lifecycle and business orchestration remain in Trading Core.

```text
LIVE  -> Broker Service technical execution -> external provider authority
PAPER -> local Trading Core simulator and settlement -> local authority
```

PAPER must not call Broker Service mutation or provider reconciliation endpoints
to determine local position truth.

Shared concepts such as idempotency, human authorization, audit, version
binding, explicit outcomes, and safe recovery may be reused. Shared concepts do
not imply shared position authority or mutation mechanics.

### 5. Internal authentication

Trading Core calls Broker Service using a service principal and an explicit
audience. User ownership remains enforced by Trading Core. Broker Service
protects sensitive technical endpoints and does not trust client-provided actor
metadata as ownership proof.

### 6. Failure semantics

Missing or stale technical facts fail closed for risk evaluation. Ambiguous LIVE
execution outcomes become `UNKNOWN` and require reconciliation before retry.
PAPER local transaction failures use rollback or an explicit recoverable local
state; they are not represented as fake external broker reconciliation.

## Consequences

* Story 0050 remains focused on local PAPER risk facts.
* Story 0051 owns broker-neutral capability and margin contracts.
* Story 0052 aligns execution concepts without moving PAPER authority.
* Market Data and Broker Service retain distinct technical responsibilities.
* A future asynchronous execution model can reuse the lifecycle without
  changing authority boundaries.

## Alternatives Rejected

### Broker Service owns all market constraints

Rejected because ADR-006 assigns instrument metadata and market constraints to
Market Data.

### Broker Service owns PAPER position state

Rejected because ADR-042 explicitly assigns PAPER position authority to Trading
Core and requires local reloadable settlement.

### Broker Service authorizes trades

Rejected because ADR-028 and ADR-030 reserve business risk authorization for the
Risk Domain and Trading Core.

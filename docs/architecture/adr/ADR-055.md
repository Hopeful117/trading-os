## Context

Trading OS currently has a hybrid market-data path:

```text
optional provider WebSocket subscription
    -> normalized TickerEvent
    -> PriceObservation

T0/T1 valuation request
    -> per-market on-demand provider acquisition when needed
    -> normalized TickerEvent
    -> PriceObservation

PriceObservation
    -> ValuationSnapshot
    -> freshness validation
    -> Trading Core
    -> deterministic Risk
```

The existing path correctly rejects incomplete or stale authoritative market
facts. The missing architecture decision is the semantic contract for an
interactive decision when the latest observation is stale or absent.

The motivating runtime evidence is:

```text
manual TradePlan
    -> ACCEPTED
    -> T0 Risk
    -> CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE
    -> CURRENT_MARKET_VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE
```

For `AAPLx/USD`, `KRAKEN`, `LINEAR_PERPETUAL`, and provider symbol
`PF_AAPLXUSD`, the provider request succeeded but the source observation was
approximately 23m57s old while the authoritative valuation policy allowed
`PT5M`.

A provider response received at the current time is not necessarily a current
market observation. In particular, a recent local `capturedAt` must not make an
old provider `effectiveAt` appear fresh.

The investigation also found 204 tradable Futures markets, with no persisted
fresh valuation at the time of observation, two stale derivative observations,
and 202 markets without an observation. Catalogue discovery and
`tradable=true` do not imply live-price readiness.

### Market Data Service

Market Data owns:

- provider symbols and product routing;
- REST and WebSocket subscriptions;
- normalization to provider-neutral market-data objects;
- source timestamps and no-lookahead validation;
- `PriceObservation` persistence;

## Decision

Trading OS SHALL formalize a provider-neutral fresh-valuation contract owned by
Market Data and consumed by Trading Core for PRE_TRADE/T0 and T1.

The contract SHALL support both:

1. reuse of a valid existing observation; and
2. bounded provider-backed acquisition when an observation is missing or stale.

The contract SHALL be expressed in terms of canonical market identity,
product type, requested instruments/assets, price usage, freshness requirement,
decision boundary, and bounded deadline. It SHALL NOT expose:

- Kraken names or payloads;
- `PF_AAPLXUSD` or other provider symbols;
- provider WebSocket feed names;
- provider-specific retry or reconnection rules.

Provider adapters remain free to implement the acquisition using REST,
WebSocket, a broker quote stream, or another provider-supported mechanism.

## Fresh Authoritative Valuation

A required valuation is **fresh and authoritative** only when all required
facts satisfy all of the following conditions:

1. The fact is available and has a usable value for its requested `PriceUse`.
2. Its source observation belongs to the requested canonical market and
   product identity.
3. Its source `effectiveAt` is not in the future relative to the final
   valuation timestamp.
4. Its source `effectiveAt` is within the requested maximum source age.
5. The observation was captured without lookahead relative to that valuation
   timestamp.
6. Any quote-to-reporting conversion legs satisfy the same source-time rules.
7. The complete valuation contains no required fact with a missing value or
   non-available status.

The timestamps have distinct meanings:

| Timestamp | Meaning | Freshness authority |
|---|---|---|
| Provider observation time / `effectiveAt` | When the provider says the quote or ticker was produced | Primary freshness authority |
| Local `capturedAt` | When Market Data received and persisted the normalized observation | Audit and no-lookahead evidence; not a substitute for `effectiveAt` |
| Valuation timestamp | The as-of time of the completed authoritative valuation | Upper bound for source timestamps |
| Request/deadline time | Decision boundary and maximum acquisition window | Controls bounded work; does not make a source quote fresh |

A provider payload received now with an old `effectiveAt` is stale. Updating
`capturedAt` does not make it fresh.

The current valuation policy remains the policy input. ADR-055 does not
increase the existing `valuation.max-observation-age` or weaken no-lookahead
semantics.

## Freshness Requirements

Freshness policy and acquisition capability are separate concerns:

```text
Market Data acquisition:
    Can a valid source observation be obtained before the deadline?

Decision/Risk policy:
    How old may that source observation be for this decision boundary?
```

Trading Core supplies the decision-boundary requirement in provider-neutral
terms. The requirement includes the maximum source age and a bounded deadline;
Market Data owns how to meet it.

The requirement may include an acquisition mode:

- `REUSE_OR_ACQUIRE` for T0, allowing a valid observation to be reused without
  an unnecessary provider call;
- `CONFIRM_OR_ACQUIRE` for T1, requiring a bounded provider-backed confirmation
  rather than silently reusing a prior T0 result.

These names are conceptual and may be represented differently in the eventual
API.

## Structured Outcomes

The provider-neutral fresh-acquisition result SHALL distinguish the following
states and SHALL avoid additional states unless implementation evidence
requires them:

### `FRESH`

Every required fact satisfies the requested source-age and completeness rules.
The returned valuation may be consumed by Trading Core and Risk.

### `STALE_PROVIDER`

Provider communication or acquisition succeeded, but the best authoritative
source observation remains older than the requested maximum age. The provider
may be reachable and its payload may be syntactically valid.

This is the incident's important distinction: a successful provider response
does not imply a fresh source observation.

### `UNAVAILABLE`

No usable authoritative observation can be obtained. Examples include missing
provider data, rejected acquisition, invalid required quote fields, unknown
market data, or a provider transport failure where no usable source result is
available.

### `TIMEOUT`

The bounded acquisition process did not resolve before its caller-provided
deadline. A timeout is not permission to return the last known stale quote.

Provider-specific diagnostic details may distinguish transport failure,
subscription rejection, or symbol mismatch inside Market Data logs and
provenance. Those details must not become provider-specific Risk states.

## Bounded Ensure-Fresh Semantics

Market Data SHALL own a bounded `ensure fresh` process conceptually equivalent
to:

```text
request fresh valuation
    -> existing required facts satisfy the policy?
       yes -> return FRESH
       no
    -> attempt provider-backed acquisition
    -> normalize and persist any provider observation
    -> required facts satisfy the policy?
       yes -> return FRESH
       no
    -> if provider acquisition is stale and an alternate supported path is
       available, establish or reuse a temporary/live subscription
    -> wait for a newer authoritative observation until the deadline
    -> return STALE_PROVIDER, UNAVAILABLE, or TIMEOUT
```

The process SHALL:

- stop at the supplied deadline;
- never retry forever;
- never sleep inside Risk;
- never accept stale data merely because the deadline was reached;
- cancel in-flight acquisition when the caller deadline is cancelled;
- release temporary subscription references on success, failure, timeout, or
  cancellation;
- preserve a fresh normalized observation when one is obtained.

Market Data may coalesce concurrent acquisition attempts for the same market
and compatible freshness requirement. A single in-flight acquisition or
subscription wait should be shareable by concurrent callers where practical.
The contract must not amplify every concurrent T0/T1 request into an
independent provider call. Distributed coordination across service instances
is not required by this ADR; local coalescing is the initial expectation.

## REST and WebSocket Acquisition

The selected provider-neutral acquisition rule is **bounded REST-first with a
WebSocket alternate path when supported**:

1. Reuse an observation that already satisfies the requested policy.
2. Attempt the provider adapter's bounded current-snapshot mechanism.
3. If the response supplies a source timestamp satisfying the policy, persist
   the normalized observation and return `FRESH`.
4. If the provider responds successfully but its source timestamp is stale,
   Market Data may reuse or establish a temporary provider stream and wait for
   a newer authoritative observation until the deadline.
5. If no qualifying observation arrives, return `STALE_PROVIDER` when stale
   provider evidence exists; otherwise return `UNAVAILABLE` or `TIMEOUT`
   according to whether the provider result or the deadline is authoritative.

Trading Core never chooses this sequence. The adapter owns whether a provider
supports the alternate path and how it maps provider acknowledgements,
reconnects, and payload timestamps.

For Kraken Futures specifically, the current REST ticker path is the initial
acquisition path and the Futures ticker WebSocket is the bounded alternate
path. Futures OHLC Charts data is not an alternate current bid/ask valuation
source.

## Dynamic Subscription Semantics

When a WebSocket alternate is used:

- an existing compatible subscription is reused;
- otherwise Market Data creates a temporary reference-counted subscription for
  the requested market and stream;
- a successful subscription acknowledgement means only that the subscription
  was accepted, not that a fresh price exists;
- readiness requires a normalized ticker whose source timestamp satisfies the
  requested freshness policy;
- a rejection is recorded as an acquisition failure and does not produce a
  fresh result;
- a disconnect ends the current wait and may be retried only within the same
  bounded deadline;
- no data before the deadline is not treated as fresh;
- temporary references are released when the acquisition completes or is
  cancelled;
- existing long-lived scanner/watchlist references are not removed by an
  interactive request.

The implementation must preserve existing provider-symbol isolation and
subscription reference counting. These mechanics remain inside Market Data.

## Coverage Model

Market Data SHALL distinguish these concepts:

```text
DISCOVERED:
    A market is known in the catalogue.

TRADABLE:
    The market state says an order may currently be attempted.

SUBSCRIBED:
    At least one active consumer has a provider stream reference.

PRICE_READY:
    A usable normalized price exists for the market.

FRESH:
    The required source timestamp satisfies the current decision policy.
```

One state must never be inferred from another. In particular:

```text
DISCOVERED != TRADABLE != SUBSCRIBED != PRICE_READY != FRESH
```

ADR-055 rejects permanent catalogue-wide Futures streaming as the default.
The approximately 204 tradable derivatives do not all need live subscriptions
merely because they exist in the catalogue.

### Passive scanner

A passive scanner may maintain continuous coverage for its configured
watchlist/universe. It must receive readiness/freshness information and exclude
markets outside its declared coverage or age policy.

### Active scanner

An active scanner may establish bounded or temporary coverage for its explicit
requested universe. It must not assume the full catalogue is price-ready.

### Interactive manual TradePlan

An interactive TradePlan must be able to request bounded fresh valuation for a
market that is not already in either scanner universe. It must not depend on
permanent catalogue-wide subscriptions.

## T0 / PRE_TRADE Semantics

T0 SHALL require a complete authoritative valuation under the existing T0
freshness policy before execution authorization can be reached.

For a manual LONG plan, the existing policy remains:

```text
CONSERVATIVE_BUY -> ask
```

For a short/sell valuation, the existing conservative side selection remains
in force. ADR-055 does not change bid/ask semantics.

T0 may reuse a source observation that already satisfies the requested age. If
it does not, it invokes bounded `REUSE_OR_ACQUIRE` behavior. T0 failure states
`STALE_PROVIDER`, `UNAVAILABLE`, and `TIMEOUT` all map to an unavailable Risk
context and cannot authorize a TradePlan.

## T1 / Execution-Time Semantics

T1 is a separate execution authorization boundary and SHALL NOT silently reuse
an old T0 valuation.

T1 SHALL use `CONFIRM_OR_ACQUIRE` behavior with a stricter execution freshness
requirement than T0 by default, supplied as provider-neutral policy. The
implementation must not hardcode a Kraken-specific age in Trading Core.

The T1 requirement is:

- a bounded provider-backed confirmation is attempted for required current
  instruments;
- the resulting source observation must satisfy the T1 maximum age;
- the source timestamp must not be future-dated relative to the completed
  valuation;
- a recent local capture cannot compensate for an old provider timestamp;
- failure to satisfy the requirement produces `STALE_PROVIDER`, `UNAVAILABLE`,
  or `TIMEOUT`, and T1 fails closed.

For Story 0101, the approved default T1 maximum source age is `PT30S`. This is
the provider-neutral execution confirmation policy. It is intentionally
separate from the T0 valuation policy, which remains `PT5M` by default.

T1 does not require a provider trade timestamp to be strictly later than every
T0 timestamp when the provider's current quote source legitimately reuses a
source timestamp. It does require a new bounded confirmation attempt and a
source observation that satisfies the stricter age policy. This avoids both
silent T0 reuse and an artificial requirement for a provider timestamp to move
on every quote check.

The stricter policy improves safety for future LIVE execution at the cost of
bounded latency and provider load. PAPER must use the same semantic contract;
PAPER does not justify weaker valuation rules.

## Timeout and Deadline Ownership

Trading Core owns the decision deadline and passes it as a provider-neutral
bounded requirement. Market Data owns the internal acquisition timeout, cleanup,
and cancellation within that deadline.

The effective acquisition deadline must not exceed either:

- the caller's decision deadline; or
- the Market Data safety maximum for one acquisition operation.

Trading Core may apply an outer request timeout, but it must not implement
provider waits, sleeps, or REST/WebSocket retry policy. No PRE_TRADE or T1
request may wait indefinitely.

## Persistence and Provenance

A successful acquisition SHALL preserve the existing normalized path:

```text
provider observation
    -> TickerEvent
    -> PriceObservation
    -> ValuationSnapshot / ValuationFact
    -> Trading Core valuation
```

An execution-only ephemeral price path is rejected unless a later ADR provides
evidence that the persisted normalized model cannot support the contract.

The authoritative valuation provenance must retain, directly or through the
existing source payload:

- canonical market identity and product type;
- provider identity;
- source effective timestamp;
- local captured timestamp;
- selected `PriceUse`;
- selected source price;
- freshness policy and maximum age;
- final valuation timestamp;
- source version;
- acquisition outcome;
- conversion-leg provenance when applicable.

Provider-specific raw payloads may remain inside Market Data provenance and
diagnostics. They must not cross into Risk as a required domain contract.

## Failure Mapping

The conceptual boundary is:

```text
Market Data FRESH
    -> complete MarketValuationPort.Snapshot
    -> Trading Core and Risk may continue

Market Data STALE_PROVIDER
    -> valuation unavailable with provider-neutral diagnostics/provenance
    -> Risk CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE

Market Data UNAVAILABLE
    -> valuation unavailable
    -> Risk CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE

Market Data TIMEOUT
    -> valuation unavailable
    -> Risk CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE
```

The existing public Risk reason `CURRENT_MARKET_VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE` remains
valid for all required current-valuation failures. Story 0101 may preserve the
specific Market Data outcome in structured diagnostics, logs, metrics, and
valuation provenance without requiring deterministic Risk rules to understand
provider-specific causes.

## Observability Requirements

Story 0101 must make the following observable for every bounded T0/T1
valuation attempt:

- decision boundary and requested market/product identity;
- requested maximum source age;
- existing observation age and status, when present;
- whether acquisition was attempted;
- acquisition mode at the provider-neutral boundary;
- final source effective timestamp and local capture timestamp;
- final freshness classification;
- elapsed acquisition time;
- structured failure outcome.

Metrics should distinguish, without blindly using high-cardinality market
labels:

- fresh acquisition success;
- provider-stale response;
- unavailable acquisition;
- acquisition timeout;
- temporary subscription readiness and failure;
- coalesced versus independent acquisitions.

## Explicit Invariants

1. Risk never calls a provider.
2. A provider response received now is not automatically fresh.
3. Provider source/effective time is authoritative for freshness.
4. A stale provider observation never becomes fresh because `capturedAt` is
   recent.
5. T0 and T1 fail closed when required valuation cannot satisfy freshness.
6. Market catalogue membership does not imply live-price readiness.
7. `DISCOVERED`, `TRADABLE`, `SUBSCRIBED`, `PRICE_READY`, and `FRESH` are not
   interchangeable states.
8. OHLC, including Futures close-price activity fallback, is not a substitute
   for current bid/ask valuation.
9. Market Facts, Opportunities, and scan results are not execution valuation
   substitutes.
10. Fresh acquisition is bounded by a caller deadline and a Market Data safety
    maximum.
11. Provider-specific acquisition remains inside Market Data adapters.
12. Successful fresh acquisition produces normalized auditable observations.
13. No operation waits indefinitely for market data.
14. T0 and T1 use the same provider-neutral valuation boundary while retaining
    separately defined decision policies.
15. Valuation acquisition retries must not be confused with execution retries
    and must not create execution side effects.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative A - Increase valuation TTL

Rejected. A larger TTL accepts older data; it does not acquire a new source
observation and would hide the incident demonstrated by `AAPLx/USD`. It would
increase execution risk and alter Risk policy without evidence that the policy
is wrong.

### Alternative B - Permanently subscribe every tradable derivative

Rejected as the default. It creates unnecessary provider load, treats the
catalogue as a scanner universe, increases reconnect and readiness complexity,
and does not generalize cleanly to future providers. Configured scanner
universes may maintain subscriptions without requiring global coverage.

### Alternative C - REST-only on-demand refresh

Rejected as sufficient. The incident proves that a reachable REST ticker can
return an old provider timestamp. REST remains a valid first acquisition path,
but it cannot be the only bounded recovery mechanism when a provider stream can
provide a newer observation.

### Alternative D - WebSocket-only valuation

Rejected as the default. It imposes startup and connection lifecycle costs on
inactive markets, complicates cold interactive requests, and excludes providers
whose authoritative snapshot mechanism is REST or another pull API.

### Alternative E - Maintained streams plus bounded ensure-fresh

Selected. It preserves efficient configured scanner coverage, supports a cold
interactive market without catalogue-wide streaming, keeps acquisition inside
Market Data, and retains fail-closed source freshness for T0/T1.

### Alternative F - Provider calls from Trading Core or Risk

Rejected. It would leak provider concerns across the service boundary, make
Risk non-deterministic and network-dependent, duplicate acquisition logic, and
violate the existing Market Data responsibility boundary.

## Consequences

Positive consequences:

- T0 and T1 acquire explicit provider-neutral fresh-valuation semantics.
- A recent local capture cannot hide a stale provider source timestamp.
- Trading Core remains independent of Kraken symbols and transport choices.
- Risk remains deterministic, read-only, and fail-closed.
- Scanner coverage becomes explicit instead of being inferred from catalogue
  membership.
- Provider-stale, unavailable, and timeout incidents become diagnosable.
- The boundary is suitable for PAPER now and future LIVE execution.
- Historical OHLC and Market Facts remain correctly separated from execution
  valuation.

Costs and risks:

- Market Data gains bounded acquisition and temporary-subscription lifecycle
  complexity.
- Cold interactive markets may add bounded latency.
- Provider rate limits and stream limits must be respected by adapters.
- Coalescing, deadline cancellation, reconnect cleanup, and provenance require
  dedicated tests.
- T1's stricter policy may reject trades that T0 approved when the market has
  become stale; this is intentional execution safety.
- A provider that genuinely remains stale will still produce a failed-closed
  decision. ADR-055 does not guarantee that a trade can proceed.

## Story 0101 Boundary

Story 0101 should be narrow and implement only:

- provider-neutral bounded fresh valuation for the existing valuation path;
- Kraken Futures REST-first acquisition with bounded WebSocket alternate where
  supported;
- explicit T0 and T1 freshness requirements;
- structured `FRESH`, `STALE_PROVIDER`, `UNAVAILABLE`, and `TIMEOUT` outcomes;
- normalized persistence and provenance;
- local acquisition coalescing and deadline cleanup;
- required logs/metrics/traces;
- unit, integration, and contract tests;
- runtime validation of the manual PAPER lifecycle.

Story 0101 explicitly excludes:

- scanner strategy redesign;
- Opportunity redesign;
- Risk-rule redesign;
- Challenge or settlement changes;
- execution lifecycle redesign;
- frontend redesign;
- Quant Research;
- live broker execution;
- permanent catalogue-wide subscription.

## Story 0101 Architectural Acceptance Criteria

The implementation must establish these behaviors:

### Fresh cached observation

```text
T0
    -> existing source observation satisfies the T0 policy
    -> no unnecessary provider refresh
    -> complete valuation
    -> Risk proceeds
```

### Missing observation

```text
T0
    -> bounded provider acquisition
    -> fresh normalized observation
    -> PriceObservation persistence
    -> complete valuation
    -> Risk proceeds
```

### Stale local observation, fresh provider available

```text
T0
    -> bounded refresh
    -> newer or otherwise policy-valid authoritative observation
    -> complete valuation
    -> Risk proceeds
```

### REST stale, alternate acquisition succeeds

```text
REST returns a valid but stale source timestamp
    -> bounded WebSocket/provider alternate
    -> policy-valid observation
    -> FRESH
    -> Risk proceeds
```

### Provider remains stale

```text
bounded acquisition exhausted
    -> STALE_PROVIDER
    -> CURRENT_MARKET_VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE / CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE
    -> no authorization
```

### Provider unavailable

```text
no usable authoritative observation
    -> UNAVAILABLE
    -> CURRENT_MARKET_VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE / CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE
    -> no authorization
```

### Acquisition timeout

```text
deadline expires before fresh acquisition resolves
    -> TIMEOUT
    -> CURRENT_MARKET_VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE / CONTEXT_UNAVAILABLE
    -> no authorization
```

### T1

T1 independently performs its `CONFIRM_OR_ACQUIRE` operation and satisfies the
stricter T1 policy. A successful T0 valuation alone is insufficient.

### Concurrency

Concurrent compatible requests for one market coalesce or reuse one bounded
acquisition/subscription wait and do not create uncontrolled provider
amplification. Cleanup occurs after the final reference is released.

### No regression

Existing Spot valuation remains provider-neutral and continues to use its
existing valid source and price semantics.

## Runtime Acceptance After Story 0101

Unit tests returning `FRESH` are not sufficient for completion. The runtime
proof must resume the manual PAPER lifecycle:

```text
CASH PAPER account
    -> ACTIVE Challenge
    -> fresh LINEAR_PERPETUAL
    -> manual TradePlan
    -> ACCEPT
    -> T0 PASS
    -> ExecutionIntent
    -> T1 PASS
    -> PAPER ENTRY
    -> ADR-054 outbox
    -> OPEN
    -> mark-to-market
    -> Challenge convergence
    -> CLOSE
    -> realized P&L
    -> post-close Challenge convergence
```

The runtime proof must retain the structured acquisition outcome and valuation
provenance for the T0 and T1 boundaries.

## Implementation Gate

This ADR is accepted and authorizes Story 0101 implementation within the
boundary defined above. The implementation must still preserve the existing
human approval workflow for the Story, and must stop if a required T1 freshness
policy cannot be resolved from existing provider-neutral configuration or an
explicit policy decision.

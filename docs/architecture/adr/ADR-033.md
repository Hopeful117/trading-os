# ADR-033 --- Active and Passive Market Intelligence Orchestration

## Status

Proposed

## Date

2026-08-20

## Context

Trading OS already provides a deterministic market-intelligence pipeline
capable of analyzing a single market through an `AnalysisExecution`.

The existing intelligence flow includes:

-   `AnalysisExecution`
-   active and passive analysis strategies
-   deterministic capabilities
-   market-data contributors
-   observation generation
-   opportunity generation
-   trade planning
-   deterministic risk evaluation
-   human validation before execution

The existing architecture therefore already contains most of the
analytical primitives required by future Active and Passive Scanner
capabilities.

The missing concern is not another market-analysis engine.

The missing concern is how Trading OS orchestrates those existing
capabilities for two fundamentally different use cases:

1.  continuous or recurring market awareness;
2.  user-triggered contextual opportunity search.

These two use cases have different orchestration semantics.

Treating both scanners simply as batch processors would lose an
important distinction in the product model.

Likewise, defining the Active Scanner merely as "analyze N markets"
would not represent its intended role in Trading OS.

The Active Scanner exists to answer a contextual trading question such
as:

> Given my current objective, account, constraints, and the current
> market state, which actionable opportunities deserve deeper
> investigation?

The Passive Scanner serves a different purpose:

> Maintain useful and sufficiently fresh market awareness across a
> configured market universe so that Trading OS can detect relevant
> changes and reuse accumulated intelligence.

This distinction must be established before introducing multi-market
Active Scan orchestration.

## Decision

Trading OS SHALL distinguish Active and Passive market-intelligence
orchestration according to their driving model.

### Active Scanner

The Active Scanner SHALL be **intention-driven**.

It represents a user-triggered search for actionable trading
opportunities under a specific trading context.

Conceptually:

``` text
User Intent
    ↓
Trading Context
    ↓
Candidate Market Universe
    ↓
Eligibility / Relevance Resolution
    ↓
Effective Scan Scope
    ↓
Targeted AnalysisExecutions
    ↓
Observations
    ↓
Opportunities
    ↓
Active Scan Result
```

The markets analyzed by an Active Scan are therefore not necessarily the
primary input of the operation.

They may instead be derived from the combination of:

-   user intent;
-   selected account;
-   broker capabilities;
-   account trading permissions;
-   applicable trading rules;
-   market tradability;
-   optional user-defined market scope;
-   current positions and relevant account context;
-   analytical capabilities available to Trading OS.

An explicit list of markets MAY still be supplied as a scope constraint.

It MUST NOT be assumed to be the final effective scan scope.

### Passive Scanner

The Passive Scanner SHALL be **batch-driven**.

Its responsibility is to maintain market awareness across a configured
market universe.

Conceptually:

``` text
Configured Market Universe
    ↓
Batch Selection
    ↓
Market Analysis
    ↓
Observations / Events / Signals
    ↓
Persisted Market Awareness
```

The Passive Scanner does not require a user trading intention for every
execution.

Its scope MAY be defined independently from the currently selected
trading account.

Examples of future passive scopes include:

-   Forex markets;
-   crypto markets;
-   configured watchlists;
-   subsets of supported markets;
-   dynamically prioritized batches.

The Passive Scanner MAY therefore monitor markets that are not currently
actionable for a specific account.

## Shared Intelligence Primitives

Active and Passive scanners SHALL reuse the existing deterministic
market-intelligence architecture.

They SHALL NOT introduce parallel implementations of:

-   technical analysis;
-   deterministic capabilities;
-   market-data contributors;
-   observations;
-   opportunity generation;
-   trade planning;
-   risk evaluation.

Conceptually:

``` text
                  Market Data
                       │
             Deterministic Capabilities
                       │
                  Observations
                       │
                 Opportunities
                  /         \
                 /           \
      Passive Orchestration   Active Orchestration
           batch-driven       intention-driven
```

The distinction between Active and Passive scanning belongs primarily to
orchestration, scope resolution, context, and purpose.

It does not imply two separate analytical engines.

## Active Scan Intent

An Active Scan SHALL originate from an explicit user intention.

The intent represents what the trader is currently trying to achieve.

Examples may eventually include:

-   search for actionable opportunities;
-   search within a specific asset class;
-   search within a watchlist;
-   search under a prop-firm account;
-   inspect opportunities compatible with a specific trading objective.

The exact intent model is outside the scope of this ADR.

However, the architecture MUST preserve the intent as part of the Active
Scan context so that downstream orchestration can be traced back to the
user's objective.

The Active Scanner MUST NOT silently transform a generic batch request
into an inferred trading intention.

## Trading Context

Active Scan scope resolution SHALL be contextual.

A Trading Context MAY contain information such as:

``` text
Trading Context
    ├── User Intent
    ├── Account
    ├── Broker
    ├── Account Rules
    ├── Broker Capabilities
    ├── Existing Positions
    ├── Available Risk Context
    ├── Market State
    └── Optional User Scope
```

Not every element is required in the first implementation.

The model MUST nevertheless preserve the architectural distinction
between:

-   the user's requested scope;
-   deterministic account/broker constraints;
-   contextual relevance;
-   the final effective scan scope.

## Market Eligibility

Trading OS SHALL distinguish **market eligibility** from **market
relevance**.

### Market Eligibility

Market eligibility answers:

> Can this market legitimately be considered actionable under the
> current trading context?

Eligibility SHOULD be deterministic.

Examples of hard exclusions include:

-   the broker does not support the instrument;
-   the account is not permitted to trade the instrument or asset class;
-   the market is not currently tradable;
-   deterministic account rules prohibit the operation;
-   Trading OS cannot safely support the required market operation.

A market that fails a hard eligibility rule SHOULD NOT consume expensive
Active Scan analysis resources.

### Market Relevance

Market relevance answers:

> Given the user's intent and current context, is deeper analysis of
> this eligible market useful right now?

Relevance is conceptually distinct from eligibility.

For example:

-   existing exposure;
-   current volatility;
-   recent passive observations;
-   user preferences;
-   current objective;
-   previously detected market events

may influence relevance without necessarily making the market
ineligible.

Future AI components MAY assist relevance interpretation.

AI MUST NOT override deterministic eligibility constraints.

## Effective Scan Scope

The Active Scanner SHALL derive an **effective scan scope** before
performing deep market analysis.

Conceptually:

``` text
Candidate Universe
        ∩
Requested Scope
        ∩
Account Eligibility
        ∩
Broker Capabilities
        ∩
Market Tradability
        ∩
System Capabilities
        ↓
Eligible Markets
        ↓
Contextual Relevance
        ↓
Effective Scan Scope
```

This model SHOULD allow Trading OS to avoid performing expensive
analysis on markets that cannot result in an actionable opportunity.

The exact eligibility and relevance policies are implementation concerns
to be introduced incrementally.

This ADR does not require a generic policy framework.

## Active Scan Aggregate

A user-triggered Active Scan SHALL be represented by a persistent
scan-level orchestration concept.

The preferred domain concept is `ActiveScan`.

`ActiveScan` represents:

> One user-triggered, contextual search for actionable trading
> opportunities.

It does NOT represent a technical-analysis algorithm.

Conceptually:

``` text
ActiveScan
    │
    ├── Intent
    ├── Trading Context Reference
    ├── Requested Scope
    ├── Effective Scope
    ├── Eligibility Decisions
    │
    └── N AnalysisExecutions
             │
             └── PipelineRun
```

The exact persisted representation of context and eligibility decisions
SHALL be determined during implementation design.

## AnalysisExecution Boundary

`AnalysisExecution` SHALL remain a single-market analysis execution.

One `ActiveScan` MAY orchestrate multiple `AnalysisExecution` instances.

Conceptually:

``` text
ActiveScan
   │
   ├── AnalysisExecution(market A)
   ├── AnalysisExecution(market B)
   └── AnalysisExecution(market C)
```

`AnalysisExecution` MUST NOT be changed into a multi-market aggregate
merely to support Active Scan orchestration.

This preserves the existing intelligence pipeline and its provenance
model.

## PipelineRun Boundary

`PipelineRun` SHALL retain its existing semantics.

It represents the execution/provenance of the intelligence pipeline
associated with one `AnalysisExecution` and pipeline version.

Conceptually:

``` text
ActiveScan
    ↓
AnalysisExecution
    ↓
PipelineRun
```

`PipelineRun` MUST NOT be reused as the multi-market Active Scan
aggregate.

Its existing technical lifecycle and cardinality are different from
Active Scan orchestration semantics.

## Active Scan Persistence

`ActiveScan` SHALL be persisted.

Persistence is required because Active Scan orchestration is
asynchronous and may involve multiple child executions.

Persisted state supports:

-   polling;
-   idempotency;
-   partial completion;
-   process restart;
-   cancellation;
-   auditability;
-   reconstruction of scan results.

An in-memory-only Active Scan model is therefore insufficient.

## Partial Failure

Active Scan orchestration SHALL preserve successful child results when
another child execution fails.

For example:

``` text
Market A → COMPLETED
Market B → FAILED
Market C → COMPLETED
```

SHALL NOT cause the results for A and C to be discarded.

The scan-level result MUST be able to represent partial completion.

Conceptually:

``` text
ActiveScan
    → PARTIALLY_COMPLETED

Results
    A → available
    B → failure diagnostic
    C → available
```

Exact lifecycle names SHALL reuse existing project conventions where
appropriate.

## Cancellation

Cancelling an Active Scan SHALL be an orchestration operation.

Cancellation SHOULD request cancellation of unfinished child
`AnalysisExecution`s.

Already completed child executions and their results MUST be preserved.

Active Scan cancellation MUST NOT behave as a rollback of completed
intelligence work.

## Idempotency

Active Scan creation SHALL be idempotent.

Retrying the same Active Scan request MUST NOT unintentionally create
duplicate sets of child analyses.

Child `AnalysisExecution` idempotency SHOULD be deterministically
derived from the scan-level operation.

The exact key format is an implementation detail.

The architectural invariant is:

``` text
one logical Active Scan request
        ↓
one ActiveScan
        ↓
one intended AnalysisExecution per selected market
```

under retries.

## Passive Intelligence Reuse

Active Scan orchestration SHOULD reuse relevant persisted intelligence
produced by Passive Scanner activity when doing so preserves correctness
and freshness requirements.

Conceptually:

``` text
Persisted Passive Intelligence
          +
Fresh Targeted Market Data
          +
User Intent
          +
Trading Context
          ↓
Active Scanner
```

An Active Scan SHOULD NOT automatically recompute information that is
already sufficiently fresh and trustworthy.

However, passive intelligence MUST NOT be treated as current merely
because it exists.

Freshness and validity requirements SHALL remain explicit.

## Opportunity Ranking

Current `OpportunityScore` values are deterministic but are not yet
proven to form a globally calibrated cross-market ranking scale.

Therefore Active Scanner V1 MUST NOT interpret raw Opportunity scores as
authoritative global rankings across markets.

The following claim is not currently justified:

``` text
OpportunityScore(BTC) > OpportunityScore(EURUSD)

therefore

BTC is the better trade.
```

Active Scan results MAY expose existing local deterministic scores.

They MUST NOT claim a globally ordered "best trade" shortlist until a
deterministic cross-market comparison policy has been explicitly defined
and validated.

## AI Boundary

AI MAY later assist with:

-   interpreting Active Scan results;
-   explaining opportunities;
-   synthesizing contextual information;
-   evaluating relevance where deterministic authority is not required;
-   presenting alternatives to the trader.

AI MUST NOT:

-   override deterministic market eligibility;
-   override deterministic account restrictions;
-   override deterministic Risk Domain decisions;
-   manufacture cross-market ranking semantics that the deterministic
    domain does not define;
-   directly authorize broker execution.

Undefined deterministic semantics MUST NOT be hidden behind an LLM
recommendation.

## Risk Authority

Active Scanner does not own trading-risk authority.

The existing deterministic Risk Domain remains authoritative for
trade-risk validation.

The existence of an opportunity in an Active Scan result does not imply
that the corresponding trade is permitted.

The downstream flow remains conceptually:

``` text
Active Scan
    ↓
Opportunity
    ↓
Trade Planning
    ↓
Deterministic Risk Validation
    ↓
Human Validation
    ↓
Execution
```

Account-aware market eligibility exists to avoid obviously irrelevant
analysis.

It MUST NOT duplicate the complete downstream Risk Engine inside the
scanner.

## Human Decision Boundary

Active Scanner is an assistance system.

Its objective is to reduce the amount of market information the trader
must inspect manually and surface contextually relevant opportunities.

It does not remove human decision authority.

Trading OS SHALL continue to distinguish:

``` text
market observation
        ↓
opportunity
        ↓
recommendation / interpretation
        ↓
trade planning
        ↓
risk validation
        ↓
human decision
        ↓
execution
```

## Consequences

### Positive

-   Active and Passive scanners receive clearly different product
    semantics.
-   Existing deterministic intelligence capabilities remain reusable.
-   Active Scanner becomes contextual rather than a generic batch
    processor.
-   Account and broker restrictions can eliminate useless analysis
    early.
-   Passive Scanner can maintain broader market awareness independently
    from a selected account.
-   Existing single-market `AnalysisExecution` semantics remain intact.
-   Existing `PipelineRun` semantics remain intact.
-   Active Scan execution becomes auditable and recoverable.
-   Partial failures can be represented without losing successful
    results.
-   The architecture naturally supports future watchlists and
    configurable market universes.
-   Passive intelligence can later reduce Active Scan computation.
-   AI can be added above deterministic eligibility and intelligence
    rather than becoming an authority layer.

### Negative

-   Active Scan requires a new persistent orchestration concept.
-   Scope resolution introduces an additional phase before analysis
    execution.
-   Account-aware eligibility introduces dependencies on account/broker
    context.
-   Partial completion and cancellation require explicit orchestration
    semantics.
-   Active and Passive Scanner orchestration cannot be represented by
    one trivial generic batch abstraction.
-   Cross-market ranking remains unavailable until a dedicated
    deterministic comparison model is established.

## Rejected Alternatives

### Alternative 1 --- Treat Active Scanner as a simple multi-market batch

Rejected.

This models implementation mechanics rather than user intent.

The Active Scanner exists to search for actionable opportunities under a
specific trading context, not merely to iterate over a list of markets.

### Alternative 2 --- Make both Active and Passive Scanner batch-driven

Rejected.

This removes the semantic distinction between contextual opportunity
search and continuous market awareness.

Passive scanning is naturally batch-driven.

Active scanning is intention-driven.

### Alternative 3 --- Make both Active and Passive Scanner intention-driven

Rejected.

Passive market awareness should not require a user trading intention for
every execution.

It must be able to continuously monitor a configured market universe.

### Alternative 4 --- Reuse PipelineRun as ActiveScan

Rejected.

`PipelineRun` is scoped to one `AnalysisExecution` and pipeline version.

It represents technical pipeline execution/provenance, not a contextual
multi-market user operation.

### Alternative 5 --- Make AnalysisExecution multi-market

Rejected.

`AnalysisExecution` already represents one market analysis and is deeply
integrated with the existing intelligence pipeline.

Multi-market orchestration belongs above it.

### Alternative 6 --- Scan every available market and filter Opportunities afterward

Rejected.

This unnecessarily consumes market-data and analytical resources for
markets that may be deterministically ineligible for the selected
account or context.

Eligibility should be evaluated before expensive targeted analysis when
possible.

### Alternative 7 --- Duplicate Risk Domain rules inside Active Scanner

Rejected.

Active Scanner may use lightweight deterministic eligibility constraints
to determine whether analysis is relevant.

Final trade-risk authority remains in the Risk Domain.

Duplicating risk authority would create conflicting business rules.

### Alternative 8 --- Use AI to select eligible markets

Rejected as an authority mechanism.

AI may assist contextual relevance in future versions.

Deterministic broker, account, market and trading restrictions remain
authoritative.

### Alternative 9 --- Use OpportunityScore directly as global ranking

Rejected.

Current Opportunity scores are not proven to provide globally calibrated
cross-market comparability.

A separate deterministic normalization/ranking decision is required
before authoritative global ranking is introduced.

## Non-Negotiable Invariants

1.  Active Scanner is intention-driven.
2.  Passive Scanner is batch-driven.
3.  Active and Passive scanners reuse shared deterministic intelligence
    primitives.
4.  `ActiveScan` represents one user-triggered contextual search for
    actionable trading opportunities.
5.  `AnalysisExecution` remains single-market.
6.  `PipelineRun` remains scoped to one `AnalysisExecution` and pipeline
    version.
7.  `ActiveScan` is persisted.
8.  Active Scan effective scope is resolved before expensive targeted
    analysis.
9.  Deterministic market eligibility is distinct from contextual market
    relevance.
10. AI MUST NOT override deterministic eligibility constraints.
11. Account-aware eligibility MUST NOT duplicate final Risk Domain
    authority.
12. Child execution failures MUST NOT invalidate successful sibling
    results.
13. Scan cancellation MUST preserve already completed child results.
14. Active Scan creation MUST be idempotent.
15. Existing Observation and Opportunity models remain authoritative
    intelligence outputs.
16. V1 MUST NOT claim globally calibrated cross-market Opportunity
    ranking.
17. AI MUST NOT manufacture undefined deterministic ranking semantics.
18. Risk Domain remains authoritative for final trade-risk validation.
19. Human validation remains part of the path from opportunity to
    execution.
20. Passive Scanner scope MAY remain independent from the currently
    selected account.
21. Relevant passive intelligence MAY be reused by Active Scanner when
    freshness and validity are established.
22. V1 does not require a generic eligibility/relevance policy
    framework.
23. V1 does not require autonomous broker execution.
24. V1 does not include Quant Research & Trading OS concerns such as
    strategy research, backtesting, portfolio optimization, or
    systematic strategy execution.

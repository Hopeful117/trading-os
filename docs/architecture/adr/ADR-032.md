# ADR-032 — Represent Trade Plan Entry Intent Explicitly

**Status:** Proposed

---

## Context

Trading OS separates trade planning, deterministic risk evaluation and trade
execution into distinct responsibilities.

Market Intelligence produces immutable Trade Plans representing the trading
intent selected during the planning phase.

Trading Core then evaluates the exact Trade Plan through the deterministic Risk
Domain before the Execution Domain is allowed to continue toward broker
execution.

Story 0004 introduces the authoritative transition from an approved
RiskEvaluation to a validated ExecutionIntent.

During implementation, a domain gap was identified.

The current Trade Plan contains:

* instrument;
* direction;
* entry price;
* quantity;
* notional;
* strategy.

However, it does not explicitly represent the intended order type or the
execution semantics associated with the planned entry.

The Execution Domain requires this information to construct its
ExecutionParameters.

Defaulting the missing information inside the Execution Domain, accepting it
from the caller during human validation, or inferring it implicitly from the
strategy would mean that the Execution Domain could execute an instruction that
was not part of the immutable Trade Plan evaluated by the Risk Domain.

This would break the guarantee that Planning, Risk and Execution operate on the
same trading intent.

---

## Problem

The current Trade Plan does not contain enough information to unambiguously
describe how the planned market entry should occur.

As a consequence, the Execution Domain cannot construct authoritative
ExecutionParameters exclusively from the Trade Plan.

Several possible workarounds exist:

* default the order type during execution;
* accept execution parameters from the caller;
* infer execution parameters from the strategy;
* extend the Trade Plan to explicitly represent its entry intent.

The first three approaches introduce business decisions after deterministic
risk evaluation and can cause the executed trade to differ from the trade that
was planned and evaluated.

The platform therefore requires an explicit architectural decision about where
entry execution semantics belong.

---

## Decision

A Trade Plan MUST explicitly contain its planned entry intent.

The entry intent is part of the immutable trading intent produced during
planning and evaluated by the Risk Domain.

It MUST contain enough information to allow the Execution Domain to construct
the corresponding ExecutionParameters without:

* selecting an order type;
* inventing execution parameters;
* deriving execution semantics from strategy names;
* accepting unvalidated execution parameters from the caller.

The Trade Plan model will introduce an explicit value object representing this
concept.

The conceptual model is:

```text
TradePlan
    |
    +-- instrument
    +-- direction
    +-- quantity
    +-- notional
    +-- strategy
    |
    +-- entryIntent
            |
            +-- orderType
            +-- price semantics required by that order type
```

The exact Java representation and naming belong to implementation design, but
the domain concept MUST remain explicit and provider-independent.

For the initial implementation, the model SHOULD support only the order types
required by the current Trading OS use cases.

The model MUST NOT introduce broker-specific concepts or values.

---

## Entry Price Semantics

The existing `entryPrice` field currently represents the planned entry price
used during Trade Plan generation and risk evaluation.

ADR-032 clarifies that entry pricing and entry execution semantics are related
but distinct concepts.

The entry intent defines how the planned entry is expected to be submitted.

For an order type requiring an explicit execution price, that price MUST be
represented by the entry intent and MUST be part of the immutable Trade Plan
evaluated by Risk.

For an order type that does not require such a price, no artificial execution
price MUST be invented by the Execution Domain.

The implementation MAY refactor the existing `entryPrice` representation to
avoid duplicated or contradictory pricing information, provided the resulting
model preserves the existing planning and risk semantics.

There MUST NOT be two independently mutable representations of the same planned
entry price.

---

## Domain Responsibilities

### Market Intelligence

Market Intelligence owns creation of the Trade Plan.

It MUST determine and persist the entry intent as part of Trade Plan creation.

It MUST NOT expose broker-specific order representations in the Trade Plan.

### Risk Domain

The Risk Domain evaluates the immutable Trade Plan including the entry intent.

Risk remains deterministic.

The Risk Domain MUST NOT select or modify the entry intent.

A change to the entry intent produces a different Trade Plan version and
requires a new RiskEvaluation.

### Trading Core

Trading Core owns the authoritative transition from the evaluated Trade Plan to
Execution.

It MUST load the persisted Trade Plan and its exact evaluated version.

It MUST NOT allow the caller to replace or override the entry intent after risk
evaluation.

### Execution Domain

The Execution Domain translates the authoritative entry intent into
ExecutionParameters.

It MUST NOT decide whether an entry should use MARKET, LIMIT or another order
type.

It MUST NOT derive order type from strategy names.

It MUST NOT silently provide default execution semantics when the Trade Plan is
incomplete.

If the entry intent cannot be translated into valid ExecutionParameters,
execution MUST fail before broker submission.

### Broker Service

Broker Service remains responsible for translating provider-independent
execution instructions into broker-specific requests.

Broker-specific order types, flags or API representations MUST remain inside
broker adapters unless explicitly promoted into the Trading OS domain through a
future architectural decision.

---

## Immutability and Versioning

Entry intent is part of the immutable Trade Plan.

Any modification to:

* order type;
* required entry price;
* or another execution-relevant property of the entry intent;

MUST produce a new Trade Plan version.

An existing RiskEvaluation MUST NOT authorize a modified entry intent.

The Execution Domain MUST use the exact entry intent contained in the Trade Plan
version referenced by the authoritative RiskEvaluation.

---

## Human Validation

Human validation does not create or modify the entry intent.

The authenticated user validates the already planned and risk-authorized Trade
Plan.

The validation request MAY identify the Trade Plan, RiskEvaluation,
BrokerAccount and idempotency information required to locate authoritative
state.

It MUST NOT provide replacement execution parameters.

Changing the desired entry semantics requires changing the Trade Plan and
performing a new RiskEvaluation before execution can continue.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

* Planning, Risk and Execution operate on the same immutable trading intent.
* Execution no longer needs to invent an order type.
* Human validation cannot modify execution semantics after risk authorization.
* Risk decisions remain meaningful because execution-relevant entry information
  is part of the evaluated Trade Plan.
* Trade Plan versioning naturally invalidates previous RiskEvaluations when
  entry intent changes.
* Broker-specific concepts remain isolated from the core domain.
* Future entry types can be introduced explicitly instead of through hidden
  conventions.

### Negative

* The Trade Plan domain model must evolve.
* Existing Trade Plan persistence must evolve.
* Trade Plan serialization and adapters must evolve.
* Trade Plan creation flows must provide an entry intent.
* Risk snapshots must include the information required to preserve the exact
  evaluated Trade Plan semantics.
* Existing tests and fixtures creating Trade Plans must be updated.
* Story 0004 implementation cannot complete until this domain gap is resolved.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Default MARKET orders during execution

Rejected.

The Execution Domain would introduce execution semantics that were not part of
the Trade Plan evaluated by Risk.

A default could therefore cause the executed trade to differ from the
authorized trading intent.

### Accept orderType and price from the caller

Rejected.

This would allow the caller to modify execution-relevant parameters after risk
evaluation and recreate the trust boundary that Story 0004 is intended to
remove.

### Derive order type from strategy

Rejected.

Strategy and order type are separate domain concepts.

Implicit mappings would introduce hidden business rules, make strategy evolution
fragile and prevent the Trade Plan from explicitly describing the trading
intent that Risk evaluates.

### Keep execution semantics exclusively inside Execution Domain

Rejected.

Execution is responsible for orchestrating and submitting an already approved
trading intent.

Selecting entry semantics at execution time would introduce a new trading
decision after risk evaluation.

---

## Scope

ADR-032 defines:

* ownership of entry intent;
* its inclusion in the immutable Trade Plan;
* its relationship with deterministic risk evaluation;
* its relationship with ExecutionParameters;
* versioning requirements;
* the prohibition of caller overrides;
* provider independence.

ADR-032 does not define:

* complete broker order capabilities;
* advanced order types;
* bracket orders;
* OCO orders;
* trailing stops;
* time-in-force policies;
* post-only or reduce-only semantics;
* position management;
* broker-specific flags;
* frontend interaction design;
* automatic order execution.

These capabilities require separate domain analysis when they become necessary.

## Resulting Flow

```text
Market Intelligence
        |
        | creates
        v
Immutable Trade Plan
+ Entry Intent
        |
        | exact version
        v
Deterministic Risk Evaluation
        |
        | authorized
        v
Human Validation
        |
        | authoritative data only
        v
Execution Intent
        |
        | translates Entry Intent
        v
Execution Parameters
        |
        v
Broker Adapter
        |
        v
Provider-specific Order
```

The same immutable entry intent therefore crosses the complete authorization
boundary without being recreated or overridden after risk evaluation.

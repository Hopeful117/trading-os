## Context

Market Data already consumes Kraken order-book snapshots and incremental updates,
reconstructs an in-memory book, exposes the latest snapshot, and publishes live
updates. This is operational state. It is not a durable record of what Trading OS
received and could later have known at an evaluation cutoff.

Story 0111 establishes a durable, strict, known-at research history for OHLC
evidence. Order Book evidence has different temporal characteristics: acquisition
is event-driven, reconstruction depends on sequence continuity, and a reconnect
can invalidate all in-memory state after the last trusted snapshot. The evidence
model must preserve those semantics rather than forcing Order Book data into the
OHLC schema or treating a current provider response as historical evidence.

Kraken documentation confirms that its book feeds provide an initial snapshot and
incremental updates, include a checksum, and require a fresh snapshot after a
reconnect. Kraken REST `Depth` returns a current book only and is not a historical
source. The Futures book feed supports the initial bounded instrument
`PF_XBTUSD`. Provider protocol details that are not represented by the current
repository DTOs, especially an explicit provider sequence number, remain an
implementation concern for Story 0112A and must not be inferred.

## Problem

Without a durable evidence boundary, a future liquidity feature could use the
latest in-memory book, silently replay across a disconnect, or consume a provider
response acquired after its evaluation cutoff. Such a feature would be neither
reproducible nor compatible with the strict temporal guarantees of ADR-057 and
ADR-058.

## Decision

Market Data owns a provider-neutral, immutable historical Order Book evidence
stream. The capture boundary is:

```text
provider WebSocket
    -> provider adapter
    -> normalized evidence
    -> checksum/trust validation
    -> durable PostgreSQL persistence
    -> deterministic reconstruction/replay
    -> Market Intelligence context and analyzers
```

The live reactive stream and the in-memory latest book remain operational views.
They are not research truth and are not used as a fallback for strict replay.

V1 persists validated provider snapshots and normalized deltas, together with
periodic full-book checkpoints produced by Trading OS. Provider snapshots and
deltas remain the canonical evidence; checkpoints are derived replay accelerators
with explicit provenance and are never treated as provider observations. V1 keeps
canonical evidence append-only and does not compact or delete it.

The initial acquisition scope is bounded to:

```text
provider: Kraken
market: BTC/USD
instrument: LINEAR_PERPETUAL
provider symbol: PF_XBTUSD
depth: 25 levels per side
```

The domain remains multi-market and multi-provider capable. The bounded scope is
an operational rollout policy, not a domain limitation.

## Evidence Types

Canonical evidence has two types:

- `SNAPSHOT`: a full provider book that establishes a trusted reconstruction
  sequence;
- `DELTA`: a normalized provider update accepted into the same valid sequence.

Sequence identity is metadata, not a third evidence type. A Trading OS capture
session creates a new `sequenceId` whenever acquisition starts from a fresh
provider snapshot. A local monotonic `sequenceOrdinal` is assigned for ordering
and idempotency diagnostics. If the provider exposes a reliable sequence field,
the adapter must preserve it as provider provenance. The implementation must not
invent a provider sequence number where the feed does not provide one.

## Provider-Neutral Representation

Canonical evidence must not expose Kraken transport DTOs. The minimum semantic
content is:

```text
marketId
provider and provider symbol provenance
evidence type
sequenceId
sequenceOrdinal
occurredAt
knownAt
depth
bids and asks as normalized price levels
checksum and checksum algorithm metadata when supplied
normalizationVersion
source/origin
content fingerprint
```

Each price level contains a positive `BigDecimal price` and a non-negative
`BigDecimal quantity`. Bids are canonically ordered from highest to lowest price;
asks from lowest to highest price. Canonical ordering is required for stable
fingerprints, deterministic replay, and deterministic downstream analysis.

The first normalization contract should be versioned as
`order-book-normalization-v1`. A semantic mapping change creates a new
normalization version and does not reinterpret existing evidence.

## Temporal Semantics

Order Book evidence carries two distinct timestamps:

```text
occurredAt = provider/source event timestamp
knownAt    = earliest defensible instant at which Trading OS accepted the evidence
```

`knownAt` is assigned at the capture boundary after parsing and trust validation.
It must not be replaced by `occurredAt`, the last level timestamp, or a derived
market interval boundary. A strict replay at cutoff `T0` may use only canonical
evidence satisfying:

```text
knownAt <= T0
```

The replay contract must also require one valid reconstruction sequence and a
trusted starting snapshot. Evidence arriving after `T0` is never eligible merely
because its provider timestamp is earlier than `T0`.

## Evidence Identity and Immutability

Every canonical record has an immutable observation identity and a deterministic
content fingerprint. The fingerprint covers semantic normalized content,
including market, evidence type, sequence identity, sequence ordinal, timestamps,
depth, levels, checksum metadata, source provenance, and normalization version.

Provider-specific message IDs or timestamps may participate in provenance and
idempotency when available, but they do not replace the canonical market identity.
Duplicate semantic evidence must be idempotent. A conflicting record is retained
as a distinct immutable observation or rejected with an explicit conflict outcome;
it must never silently overwrite an earlier record.

Raw provider payloads are not part of the canonical research contract in V1. The
adapter may emit bounded diagnostic logging or a separately governed diagnostic
artifact later, but canonical persistence remains normalized and provider-neutral
to avoid coupling research truth to Kraken JSON.

## Reconstruction Semantics

A valid sequence has this shape:

```text
SNAPSHOT(sequence N)
    -> DELTA(sequence N)
    -> DELTA(sequence N)
    -> checkpoint(sequence N)
    -> DELTA(sequence N)
```

The snapshot establishes the initial state. Each eligible delta is applied in
canonical order and only after the adapter has accepted its trust conditions. A
checkpoint stores a complete reconstructed book and references the sequence and
source evidence range that produced it. It is a derived acceleration artifact,
not a replacement for canonical snapshot/delta evidence.

The first V1 checkpoint policy is operationally bounded rather than optimized:
persist a checkpoint at most every five minutes while a sequence is active and
persist one immediately before a sequence is closed when practical. The exact
checkpoint scheduler and back-pressure behavior belong to Story 0112A.

The replay service must select the latest valid snapshot or checkpoint in one
sequence that is eligible at `T0`, then apply only eligible deltas from that same
sequence with `knownAt <= T0`. It returns the reconstructed state and provenance,
not only the levels.

## Checksum, Gaps, and Trust

Checksum validation is part of the capture trust boundary. A checksum failure is
not eligible for research reconstruction. The failed message may be recorded as
a bounded diagnostic/rejection event if the implementation needs operational
forensics, but it is not canonical trusted evidence.

The current in-memory book is invalidated when any of the following occurs:

- checksum validation fails;
- a provider sequence gap or out-of-order update is detected;
- a reconnect or transport interruption creates an unknown update gap;
- the snapshot is malformed or cannot be validated;
- persistence cannot durably acknowledge the evidence needed to continue.

The system fails closed. It never interpolates missing deltas, merges deltas from
before and after a gap, or reuses an untrusted in-memory state. Recovery requires
discarding the untrusted state, obtaining a new valid provider snapshot, and
starting a new `sequenceId`.

Where the provider does not expose an explicit sequence number, the implementation
must use connection boundaries, checksum validation, and local accepted ordering
without claiming that every network-level gap is observable. That limitation must
be exposed in evidence provenance and operational metrics rather than hidden.

## Persistence and Retention

PostgreSQL remains sufficient for the bounded V1 pilot of one market at depth 25.
No Kafka, ClickHouse, TimescaleDB, Redis Streams, or new service is introduced by
this decision. Event volume must be measured during Story 0112A before expanding
market scope or depth.

V1 retains all canonical validated snapshots and deltas. Checkpoints improve
replay cost but do not authorize deletion or silent compaction of the source
evidence. Retention and archival are deferred until observed event volume,
storage growth, and research reproducibility requirements have been measured.
This is an explicit bounded-pilot decision, not an assumption that unlimited
catalog-wide capture is sustainable.

## Capture Frequency and Depth

Ingestion is event-driven by the provider WebSocket. It must not be converted into
OHLC-style polling or represented as artificial candles. Full-book checkpoint
persistence frequency is independent from event ingestion and from the later
research `FeatureSnapshot` cadence.

V1 subscribes to depth 25 per side. This provides more liquidity context than the
existing depth-10 baseline while keeping the pilot bounded. A future story may
select another depth only through an explicit acquisition policy and a compatible
normalization/provenance decision.

## Replay Contract

Future consumers may ask:

> What latest valid Order Book state could Trading OS reconstruct from evidence
> known at evaluation cutoff `T0`?

The replay result must identify:

- the selected sequence;
- the starting snapshot or checkpoint;
- the eligible evidence range;
- the last applied sequence ordinal/provider sequence when available;
- checksum/trust status;
- `occurredAt` and `knownAt` boundaries;
- freshness metadata for consumer policy;
- explicit unavailable or invalid reasons.

If no complete valid reconstruction exists at `T0`, replay returns an explicit
unavailable/invalid result. It must not use current provider state, evidence with
`knownAt > T0`, a state across a known sequence break, or fabricated missing
updates.

Freshness is a consumer policy, not an evidence identity rule. A consumer may
reject an otherwise valid replay as stale according to its explicit policy without
changing the historical evidence.

## Boundaries and Compatibility

Market Data owns provider acquisition, normalization, trust validation, durable
evidence, and deterministic reconstruction. Market Intelligence owns the
deterministic interpretation of a replayed `OrderBookContext`, such as future
spread, depth, imbalance, or microstructure analysis. Broker, Risk, Execution,
Frontend, and Gateway responsibilities do not change.

Future Order Book features must be traceable to the exact replay result and source
evidence fingerprints that produced them. They must not mutate `feature-projection-v1`
to add new semantic membership. A future feature schema change requires a new
projection version, likely `feature-projection-v2`, under ADR-060 semantics.

ADR-063 does not define final liquidity formulas. Absorption remains deferred
because it requires Order Book evolution together with trade-flow evidence. The
evidence model must not prevent that future capability, but Story 0112A remains
narrowly focused on acquisition, persistence, trust, and replay.

## Operational Considerations

The implementation must expose or record at least:

- last valid provider snapshot time;
- last accepted `knownAt`;
- active sequence ID;
- reconnect count;
- checksum failure count;
- sequence reset count;
- persisted snapshot and delta counts;
- checkpoint count and replay duration;
- current snapshot age;
- capture and persistence failures.

On application restart, the in-memory book is not trusted as durable state. The
capture process resumes from a fresh provider snapshot and a new sequence unless a
future protocol-specific decision proves safe continuation. Database failure must
stop or quarantine canonical acquisition rather than acknowledging evidence that
cannot be replayed.

Order Book data is public market data. This ADR does not add Spring Security or
JWT requirements to Market Data and preserves the existing service-security
boundary.

## Relationship with Story 0111

OHLC and Order Book become parallel evidence histories:

```text
OHLC evidence       -> knownAt/cutoff -> strict research snapshots/outcomes
Order Book evidence -> sequence/replay/cutoff -> future liquidity snapshots
```

They share concepts such as `knownAt`, immutability, provider neutrality,
fingerprints, provenance, and replay cutoffs. They do not share the same physical
evidence schema or acquisition cadence. Order Book must not be forced into the
OHLC persistence model merely to reuse tables or schedulers.

Historical Kraken Order Book data cannot currently be assumed recoverable later.
Once the evidence design is human-approved and Story 0112A is validated, bounded
capture should be enabled promptly for research value without weakening temporal
truth or bypassing testing.

## Alternatives Considered

### Persist full normalized snapshots only

Rejected for V1. It simplifies replay but loses the event history needed to audit
reconstruction, diagnose checksum/sequence failures, and preserve what the feed
actually delivered between snapshots.

### Persist snapshots and normalized deltas without checkpoints

Rejected for V1. It preserves evidence but allows replay cost to grow without a
bounded acceleration point.

### Persist every reconstructed full book

Rejected for V1. It duplicates state on every update, increases storage and write
load, and obscures the distinction between provider evidence and derived state.

### Use a streaming or time-series infrastructure platform

Rejected for the bounded pilot. The expected initial scope is one market at depth
25, and PostgreSQL already owns the related durable research evidence. A scale
decision requires measured event volume rather than speculative infrastructure.

### Fetch historical books from Kraken REST during replay

Rejected. REST `Depth` is current state, not historical evidence, and would violate
the strict replay contract.

## Consequences

Positive consequences:

- historical Order Book state becomes reproducible and auditable;
- reconnects and checksum failures cannot silently contaminate research;
- provider payloads remain isolated from the domain model;
- replay can enforce `knownAt <= T0` and valid sequence boundaries;
- future liquidity features can preserve exact evidence lineage;
- the initial implementation remains bounded and PostgreSQL-based.

Costs and risks:

- event-level persistence can grow quickly and must be measured;
- provider feeds without explicit sequence numbers cannot expose every network gap;
- capture must handle back-pressure and persistence failure without acknowledging
  untrusted continuation;
- Story 0112A requires integration tests for reconnect, checksum, idempotency,
  and replay cutoff semantics before broader capture.

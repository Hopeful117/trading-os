# ADR-067 - Bounded Order Book Evidence Retention and Replay Promotion

|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |

---

## 1. Context

ADR-063 established immutable normalized Order Book snapshots and deltas,
sequence-specific reconstruction, strict `knownAt` replay, and PostgreSQL storage
for the bounded pilot. It intentionally deferred retention and archival while event
volume and reproducibility requirements were measured.

The bounded pilot has now demonstrated that canonical evidence can grow by several
gigabytes per day. Indefinite raw retention is therefore not a safe operational
default. At the same time, deleting evidence by age alone could invalidate frozen
research populations, historical execution replay, or future audit requirements.

ADR-066 requires frozen research populations to bind explicit immutable snapshot and
outcome identities. This decision extends that principle to the Order Book evidence
needed to produce and reproduce those snapshots and replay results.

## 2. Decision

Trading OS uses lifecycle-aware bounded retention. Promoted immutable replay
artifacts are sufficient for the reproducibility of a frozen research dependency;
raw Order Book evidence does not need to be retained indefinitely once every
protected dependency has been released.

Raw evidence remains protected while it is required by:

- an active or recoverable capture sequence;
- an unpromoted replay or research operation;
- a frozen `FeatureSnapshot`, `ResearchSample`, dataset manifest, or execution
  replay dependency;
- an unresolved diagnostic or integrity investigation;
- an explicit legal, operational, or human retention hold.

Raw evidence may become eligible for retirement only after all of the following are
true:

```text
sequence is no longer ACTIVE
AND a trusted replay boundary has been promoted when reproducibility requires it
AND all dependent research/replay artifacts reference the promoted boundary
AND no dependency or retention hold remains unresolved
AND provenance and integrity metadata are preserved
```

Eligibility is not deletion. A separate idempotent retirement operation must record
the decision, the protected artifact or release event, the evidence range, and the
normalization/provenance versions before physical deletion or compaction is allowed.

## 3. Replay Promotion Contract

A promoted replay artifact is an immutable, content-addressed record containing at
least:

- market and instrument identity;
- provider and provider-symbol provenance;
- depth and normalization version;
- selected sequence identity;
- trusted starting snapshot or checkpoint identity;
- eligible evidence range and final sequence ordinal/provider sequence when
  available;
- evaluation cutoff and relevant `knownAt` boundaries;
- checksum and trust status;
- source evidence fingerprints;
- creation time and promotion reason;
- schema and artifact-version identifiers.

Promotion must fail closed if the replay is incomplete, invalid, crosses a sequence
boundary, or contains evidence with `knownAt` after its cutoff. Promotion must be
atomic and idempotent for the same semantic replay identity.

A promoted artifact is not a replacement for the current operational book and does
not authorize reconstruction from an invalidated sequence. It is a frozen research
or replay boundary whose provenance must remain independently verifiable.

## 4. Dependency and Release Semantics

Research and replay consumers must declare or persist their evidence dependency.
Dependency identity must be stable and portable; generated database identifiers
alone are insufficient.

At minimum, dependency records must distinguish:

```text
PROTECTED       evidence cannot be retired
PROMOTED        required replay artifact is immutable and verified
RELEASED        consumer no longer requires raw evidence
HOLD            human or operational investigation blocks retirement
```

An unresolved dependency blocks retirement. Missing retention metadata is treated as
protected, not as permission to delete. Release must be explicit or derived from a
documented terminal lifecycle event; an age threshold cannot implicitly release a
research dependency.

Invalidated sequences receive the same fail-closed treatment. They may be retained
for a configurable diagnostic period, but that period must not override an active
hold or an unresolved integrity investigation.

## 5. Storage-Pressure Behavior

Storage pressure is an operational safety signal, not a reason to weaken replay
truth. The system must expose thresholds and stop or quarantine canonical capture
before persistence becomes unreliable.

Required behavior:

- report filesystem and database headroom;
- report last successful canonical evidence persistence;
- expose active sequence and capture health;
- stop or quarantine acquisition when canonical persistence cannot be acknowledged;
- never acknowledge evidence that was not durably persisted;
- never substitute live provider state or partial evidence in strict replay;
- surface an explicit degraded or unavailable state to consumers.

Cleanup must be bounded, observable, resumable, and idempotent. Cleanup must not
retire evidence solely because storage pressure exists.

## 6. Reproducibility Boundary

For a frozen research population, reproducibility is defined by its immutable
selected facts, including the promoted Order Book replay artifacts and their
versions. Re-running the current implementation against newly available raw data
does not change the frozen population.

Future reprojection or recomputation with a different normalization, replay, or
feature algorithm creates a new immutable artifact and version. It does not mutate
or reinterpret the existing promoted artifact.

This decision does not require indefinite preservation of raw provider payloads.
Raw provider payloads remain outside the canonical V1 research contract unless a
future decision explicitly adds a governed diagnostic archive.

## 7. Migration and Existing Data

Existing canonical evidence is not deleted, compacted, or reclassified by this ADR.
The current approximately 36.2 million Order Book evidence rows remain protected
until a separate implementation story defines and validates migration, dependency
backfill, and retirement behavior.

The first implementation must establish dependency metadata conservatively. When
provenance or dependency status is unknown, the evidence remains protected.

## 8. Alternatives Rejected

### Indefinite Raw Retention

Rejected as the default because event-level capture can grow without bound and has
already demonstrated a material storage saturation risk. It remains available only
as an explicit retention hold or future product requirement.

### Fixed Age-Based Deletion

Rejected because age does not establish that a frozen research population, replay,
or diagnostic investigation has released its evidence dependency.

### Delete Immediately After Checkpoint Creation

Rejected because a checkpoint alone does not prove that all replay provenance,
research dependencies, and integrity diagnostics have been preserved.

### Compact Canonical Evidence Without Promotion

Rejected because compaction without an immutable promoted boundary can change the
available reconstruction path and weaken auditability.

### Automatic Cleanup Under Storage Pressure

Rejected because operational pressure must not silently change research truth.
Pressure triggers degraded capture and explicit cleanup workflows, not implicit
retirement.

## 9. Consequences

Positive:

- frozen research remains reproducible after raw evidence retirement;
- storage growth becomes bounded by protected dependencies and configured budgets;
- replay provenance is explicit and independently verifiable;
- missing metadata fails closed and protects evidence;
- cleanup can be tested as an idempotent lifecycle operation;
- existing Market Data, Market Intelligence, Risk, Broker, and Execution ownership
  boundaries remain unchanged.

Trade-offs:

- promotion and dependency tracking add persistence and lifecycle complexity;
- research consumers must participate in evidence dependency management;
- historical recomputation with new logic requires new artifacts and retained raw
  evidence or an explicit hold;
- cleanup cannot safely proceed until unknown dependencies are resolved;
- storage budgets, diagnostic periods, and release events require configuration and
  operational monitoring.

## 10. Implementation Sequence

Implementation is deliberately split from this decision:

1. Add replay-promotion and dependency contracts without deleting evidence.
2. Add immutable promotion validation and provenance checks.
3. Add protected/released/hold lifecycle handling and conservative backfill.
4. Add bounded cleanup with dry-run, metrics, audit records, and idempotency.
5. Add storage-pressure gates and sustained runtime validation.

The existing Story 0112A remains focused on acquisition and replay foundation. A
new retention story is required before implementing deletion, compaction, or
archival behavior.

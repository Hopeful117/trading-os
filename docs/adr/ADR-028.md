# ADR-028 — Risk Engine Architecture

**Status** : Accepted

**Date** : 2026-07-30

**Decision Makers** :
- Project Owner
- Architecture

---

# Context

The Trading OS aims to become an AI-assisted discretionary trading platform where the user remains the final decision maker while the platform provides deterministic risk management, market analysis and intelligent assistance.

Among all platform components, the Risk Engine is one of the most critical.

Unlike analytical or advisory components, the Risk Engine is responsible for protecting trading capital and enforcing trading constraints before any order reaches a broker.

A failure of this component could directly result in:

- violation of prop firm rules,
- excessive financial losses,
- inconsistent platform behavior,
- non reproducible decisions,
- loss of user trust.

For this reason, risk management cannot depend on AI reasoning, heuristics or opaque decision making.

The Risk Engine must always behave deterministically and produce reproducible, explainable and auditable decisions.

This ADR defines the architecture of the Risk Domain, its responsibilities, evaluation pipeline, rule model and design principles.

---

# Problem

Many trading applications implement risk management as a collection of validations scattered across services.

Typical problems include:

- duplicated calculations;
- business rules mixed with execution logic;
- inconsistent validations;
- difficult testing;
- hidden side effects;
- poor explainability;
- impossible auditing.

As Trading OS grows to support multiple brokers, configurable prop firm rules, AI assistance and advanced analytics, this approach becomes impossible to maintain.

The platform therefore requires a dedicated Risk Domain with clearly defined responsibilities and deterministic behavior.

---

# Decision Drivers

The architecture must satisfy the following goals.

## Determinism

The same inputs must always produce the same outputs.

No hidden state may influence a decision.

---

## Explainability

Every decision must be understandable.

The platform must always be capable of answering:

> Why was this TradePlan accepted or rejected?

---

## Auditability

Every evaluation must be reproducible long after execution.

The evaluation context, applied rules, metrics and decision must remain traceable.

---

## Traceability

Every decision must be linked to:

- evaluation request;
- snapshots;
- rule versions;
- policy versions;
- engine version;
- timestamps.

---

## Separation of Responsibilities

Risk evaluation must remain independent from:

- trade planning;
- order execution;
- AI reasoning;
- broker communication;
- dashboard presentation.

---

## Capital Protection

The primary objective is protecting capital.

The engine is intentionally conservative.

Whenever uncertainty exists, authorization must not be granted.

---

# Architectural Vision

The Risk Engine is not a validation helper.

It is not a rule scripting engine.

It is not an execution engine.

It is not an AI component.

Instead, it is a deterministic business engine responsible for evaluating trading risk using immutable business context and configurable business constraints.

Its purpose is to determine whether a proposed trading action complies with every applicable rule before execution.

---

# Architectural Principles

The following principles apply to the entire Risk Domain.

## Immutable Inputs

Risk evaluation always operates on immutable snapshots.

No component may modify the evaluation context during execution.

---

## Stateless Evaluation

The Risk Engine does not maintain mutable internal state.

Every evaluation is independent.

---

## Read-Only Domain

The engine never modifies:

- accounts;
- positions;
- portfolios;
- TradePlans.

It only evaluates.

---

## No Repository Access

The Risk Engine never queries repositories.

It never calls external services.

It never loads additional information.

All required information must already exist inside the RiskEvaluationContext.

---

## Pure Business Logic

The Risk Domain contains only business logic.

Infrastructure concerns remain outside the engine.

---

## Fail-Closed

Whenever required information cannot be evaluated safely, authorization must not be granted.

---

# Decision 1 — Risk Engine Responsibilities

## Decision

The Risk Engine is defined as a deterministic authorization engine.

Its responsibility is limited to evaluating whether a proposed trading action satisfies every applicable risk constraint.

The engine never creates, modifies or executes trades.

Instead, it evaluates immutable business information and produces a deterministic decision.

---

## Responsibilities

The Risk Engine is responsible for:

- evaluating TradePlans;
- evaluating account state;
- evaluating portfolio state;
- validating configurable business rules;
- producing explainable decisions;
- generating structured evaluation results.

---

## Non Responsibilities

The Risk Engine is explicitly **not** responsible for:

- creating TradePlans;
- modifying TradePlans;
- discovering trading opportunities;
- executing broker orders;
- managing positions;
- portfolio optimization;
- AI reasoning;
- market prediction.

These responsibilities belong to dedicated platform components.

---

## Outputs

Every evaluation produces a RiskValidationResult.

Possible business decisions are:

- APPROVED
- APPROVED_WITH_WARNINGS
- REJECTED

The result contains significantly more information than a simple boolean and represents a complete business decision.

---

# Decision 2 — Rule-Based Architecture

## Decision

Risk evaluation is implemented through independent RiskRule components.

Each rule evaluates one business constraint only.

Rules never depend on each other.

Rules never modify business state.

Rules remain individually testable.

---

## Architecture

```
Risk Engine
        │
        ▼
Risk Rule Registry
        │
        ▼
RiskRule
        │
        ▼
RiskRuleResult
        │
        ▼
RiskValidationResult
```

---

## Rule Characteristics

Every RiskRule must be:

- deterministic;
- independent;
- configurable;
- reusable;
- immutable during evaluation;
- individually testable.

---

## Rule Categories

Rules may belong to categories such as:

- Position
- Account
- Portfolio
- Prop Firm
- Session
- Market
- Compliance
- Custom

Categories improve organization only.

They do not change evaluation behavior.

---

# Decision 3 — Risk Rule Hierarchy

## Decision

Risk constraints originate from multiple authorities.

The Risk Engine never evaluates raw configuration.

Instead, it evaluates a resolved EffectiveRiskRuleSet produced before evaluation.

---

## Authority Levels

The architecture recognizes the following hierarchy.

```
Platform Safety Rules

↓

Broker Constraints

↓

Prop Firm Rules

↓

Account Rules

↓

User Rules

↓

Strategy Preferences
```

Higher authorities always take precedence.

Lower authorities may strengthen constraints.

They may never weaken higher-level safety constraints.

---

## Effective Rule Set

Configured rule sets are composed into one immutable EffectiveRiskRuleSet before evaluation.

```
Configured Rule Sets

↓

Conflict Resolution

↓

EffectiveRiskRuleSet

↓

Risk Engine
```

This completely separates configuration from evaluation.

---

## Overrides

Overrides must remain:

- explicit;
- temporary;
- authorized;
- auditable.

Platform safety rules cannot be overridden.

---

# Decision 4 — RiskEvaluationContext

## Decision

Risk evaluation operates exclusively on a complete immutable RiskEvaluationContext.

The Risk Engine never collects information itself.

Instead, all required business information is assembled beforehand.

---

## Context Builder

A dedicated RiskEvaluationContextBuilder gathers information from the platform.

```
Account Service

Portfolio Service

Broker Service

Market Data Service

Rule Service

↓

RiskEvaluationContextBuilder

↓

Immutable RiskEvaluationContext

↓

Risk Engine
```

The engine therefore remains completely independent from infrastructure.

---

## Context Contents

The evaluation context may include:

- EffectiveRiskRuleSet
- TradingContext
- TradePlan
- AccountSnapshot
- PortfolioSnapshot
- Open Positions
- Pending Orders
- Market State
- Prices
- Equity
- Balance
- Margin
- Drawdown
- ValidationMode

The context represents one coherent business snapshot.

---

## Validation Modes

The Risk Engine supports multiple evaluation modes.

Examples include:

- PRE_TRADE
- POSITION_MONITORING
- PORTFOLIO_MONITORING
- ACCOUNT_MONITORING
- SIMULATION

Each mode evaluates the same business model while adapting the available information.

---

## Monitoring

The Risk Engine does not actively monitor accounts.

Monitoring belongs to dedicated services.

```
Risk Monitoring Service

↓

Risk Engine

↓

RiskEvaluationResult

↓

Alert Service
```

This separation keeps evaluation deterministic while allowing continuous monitoring elsewhere.

---

# Consequences (Part 1)

These first architectural decisions establish the foundations of the Risk Domain.

The architecture now provides:

- deterministic evaluations;
- immutable business context;
- configurable rule hierarchy;
- separation between configuration and evaluation;
- strict responsibility boundaries;
- infrastructure-independent business logic.

Subsequent decisions will define:

- RiskRule internal model;
- RiskValidationResult;
- Risk Metrics;
- Projection Engine;
- Risk Policies;
- Auditability;
- Evaluation pipeline.

# Decision 5 — RiskRule Domain Model

## Decision

A RiskRule represents one atomic business constraint.

A rule is **not** an algorithm, a workflow or a collection of validations.

Its sole responsibility is evaluating one business constraint using the immutable RiskEvaluationContext.

Rules never reconstruct system state.

Rules never depend on other rules.

---

## RiskRule Structure

Every RiskRule is composed of the following concepts.

```
RiskRule

├── Identity
├── Category
├── Severity
├── Configuration
├── Applicability
├── Evaluation
├── Explanation
└── Metadata
```

---

## Identity

Each rule owns a globally unique identifier.

Examples include:

- MAX_POSITION_RISK
- FTMO_DAILY_DRAWDOWN
- MAX_PORTFOLIO_EXPOSURE

Identifiers remain stable across versions.

---

## Configuration

Rules are parameterized.

Business constraints must never be hardcoded.

Example:

```
Maximum Position Risk

Maximum Allowed Risk

1%
```

The same implementation may therefore be reused by multiple brokers, prop firms or user profiles.

---

## Applicability

Every rule decides whether it applies to the current evaluation.

```
supports(context)
```

Rules that do not apply produce a NOT_APPLICABLE result.

This status is preserved for auditability.

---

## Evaluation

Rules evaluate immutable business information.

```
evaluate(context)
```

Evaluation must:

- remain deterministic;
- avoid side effects;
- never modify the context;
- never invoke external services.

---

## Explanation

Rules always explain their result.

Explanations must be structured rather than free-form.

Example:

```
Rule

FTMO Daily Drawdown

Maximum

5.00%

Current

5.18%

Exceeded

0.18%
```

---

## Complex Rules

Some business constraints are inherently complex.

Examples include:

- Daily Drawdown
- Portfolio Heat
- Correlation Exposure
- Maximum Exposure

Complexity belongs inside dedicated business objects and calculators.

It must never leak into the Risk Engine itself.

---

## Design Principle

RiskRules never reconstruct business state.

Instead, they reason over immutable business snapshots and precomputed metrics.

---

# Decision 6 — RiskValidationResult

## Decision

Risk evaluation produces structured business results rather than boolean values.

Every rule produces an individual RiskRuleResult.

The Risk Engine aggregates these into one RiskValidationResult.

---

## RiskRuleResult

```
RiskRuleResult

├── RuleId
├── Status
├── Severity
├── Explanation
├── Metrics
├── Timestamp
└── Metadata
```

---

## Status

Supported statuses are:

- PASS
- WARNING
- FAILURE
- NOT_APPLICABLE

These statuses describe the outcome of one individual rule.

---

## Severity

Severity describes business importance.

Typical values include:

- INFO
- WARNING
- BLOCKING

Severity is independent from evaluation status.

---

## Metrics

Each rule may expose structured business metrics.

Examples include:

- Allowed Risk
- Current Risk
- Remaining Daily Loss
- Exposure Percentage

These metrics are reused by dashboards, analytics and audit reports.

---

## RiskValidationResult

The engine aggregates every rule result into one immutable object.

```
RiskValidationResult

├── Decision
├── RuleResults
├── Violations
├── Warnings
├── Global Metrics
├── Evaluation Mode
├── Evaluation Duration
├── Timestamp
└── Trace Information
```

---

## Business Decisions

Possible decisions are:

- APPROVED
- APPROVED_WITH_WARNINGS
- REJECTED

Business decisions are determined only after every applicable rule has been evaluated.

---

## Risk Evaluation Report

The RiskValidationResult represents the business outcome.

Presentation components may build richer reports from this information.

Typical report contents include:

- explanations;
- metrics;
- recommendations;
- audit information;
- visualization.

The report itself remains outside the Risk Engine.

---

# Decision 7 — Risk Metrics

## Decision

Business metrics are separated from business rules.

Rules consume metrics.

They do not calculate them.

---

## Motivation

Many business constraints rely on identical financial calculations.

Duplicating those calculations inside multiple rules would produce:

- inconsistencies;
- duplicated code;
- difficult maintenance;
- poor testing.

The platform therefore introduces a dedicated Risk Metrics layer.

---

## Metric Categories

Three families of metrics are recognized.

### Observed Metrics

Observed metrics describe the current system state.

Examples include:

- Balance
- Equity
- Floating PnL
- Closed PnL
- Used Margin
- Free Margin

---

### Projected Metrics

Projected metrics represent the hypothetical account state after TradePlan execution.

Examples include:

- Projected Drawdown
- Projected Exposure
- Projected Margin Usage
- Projected Portfolio Heat

---

### Derived Metrics

Derived metrics summarize business information.

Examples include:

- Remaining Daily Loss
- Remaining Exposure
- Risk Utilization
- Portfolio Heat

---

## Architectural Principle

Rules consume immutable metrics.

They never recreate financial calculations themselves.

---

# Decision 8 — Projection Engine

## Decision

The platform introduces a dedicated Projection Engine.

Its responsibility is producing projected business metrics from a hypothetical TradePlan.

---

## Motivation

Many risk constraints depend on future account state rather than current account state.

Examples include:

- projected drawdown;
- projected exposure;
- projected margin usage.

Without a dedicated projection layer, every rule would have to perform identical simulations.

---

## Architecture

```
Snapshots

↓

Observed Metrics

↓

Projection Engine

↓

Projected Metrics

↓

Derived Metrics

↓

Risk Rules
```

---

## Responsibilities

The Projection Engine:

- simulates TradePlan impact;
- computes projected account state;
- computes projected portfolio state;
- exposes reusable projected metrics.

---

## Non Responsibilities

The Projection Engine does not:

- authorize trades;
- evaluate rules;
- execute trades;
- predict markets.

It only computes deterministic hypothetical business state.

---

## Design Principle

Projection is performed once.

Every rule consumes identical projected metrics.

This guarantees:

- consistency;
- reproducibility;
- simplified testing;
- improved maintainability.

---

# Consequences (Part 2)

The Risk Domain now separates:

- business state;
- business metrics;
- business projections;
- business constraints;
- business decisions.

Every component owns one responsibility only.

Rules become small, reusable and deterministic while financial calculations remain centralized and reusable across the platform.


# Decision 9 — Risk Policies

## Decision

Risk rules remain atomic and independent.

The platform introduces RiskPolicy as a configuration concept responsible for defining which rules apply in a particular business context.

Policies never alter the behavior of individual rules.

They only determine the effective set of constraints to evaluate.

---

## Motivation

Risk constraints originate from multiple authorities:

- platform safety;
- broker requirements;
- prop firm rules;
- account configuration;
- user preferences.

These constraints must be combined consistently before evaluation.

The Risk Engine must never resolve policy conflicts during rule execution.

---

## RiskPolicy Responsibilities

A RiskPolicy is responsible for:

- grouping business rules;
- configuring rule parameters;
- defining applicability;
- identifying the policy source;
- participating in rule set resolution.

A policy never evaluates risk.

---

## Effective Risk Rule Set

Before evaluation begins, every applicable policy is resolved into one immutable EffectiveRiskRuleSet.

```
Configured Policies

↓

Hierarchy Resolution

↓

Conflict Resolution

↓

EffectiveRiskRuleSet

↓

Risk Engine
```

The Risk Engine therefore evaluates only one consistent rule set.

---

## Mandatory Rules

All applicable blocking rules are mandatory.

A blocking rule cannot be compensated by another successful rule.

The platform intentionally avoids generic logical operators such as:

- OR
- NOT
- "3 of 5"

Such operators are incompatible with the platform's capital protection philosophy.

Risk evaluation is based on mandatory compliance rather than acceptable compromise.

---

## Architectural Principle

RiskPolicies configure.

RiskRules evaluate.

The Risk Engine decides.

---

# Decision 10 — Auditability, Traceability and Explainability

## Decision

Every Risk Evaluation must be reproducible, explainable, auditable and fully traceable.

These properties are architectural requirements rather than optional features.

---

## Motivation

The purpose of auditability is not only debugging.

It enables:

- user confidence;
- historical analysis;
- deterministic replay;
- AI-assisted analytics;
- trading review;
- long-term learning.

The Risk Engine therefore produces structured business artifacts rather than transient execution results.

---

## Evaluation Artifact

Every evaluation generates one immutable RiskValidationResult.

This object represents the official business decision.

It includes:

- evaluation identifier;
- timestamps;
- engine version;
- policy versions;
- rule versions;
- evaluation mode;
- context information;
- metrics;
- rule results;
- final decision.

---

## Explainability

Every business decision must answer:

> Why was this decision produced?

The explanation is generated from structured business information.

No business decision may rely on opaque reasoning.

---

## Traceability

Every evaluation is linked to:

- RiskEvaluationRequest;
- EffectiveRiskRuleSet;
- RiskEvaluationContext;
- Snapshots;
- Metrics;
- Rule Results;
- Decision.

Every artifact can therefore be traced back to its origin.

---

## Learning Philosophy

Producing educational feedback is **not** the responsibility of the Risk Engine.

Instead, the Risk Engine produces rich, deterministic and explainable business information.

Other platform components such as:

- Analytics;
- Dashboard;
- AI Engine;
- Historical Analysis;

consume this information to help the user understand past decisions and continuously improve trading discipline.

Learning therefore emerges from the collaboration of multiple platform components rather than from the Risk Engine itself.

---

## Architectural Principle

Business components produce trustworthy knowledge.

The Trading OS transforms this knowledge into user understanding.

---

# Decision 11 — Risk Evaluation Pipeline

## Decision

Risk evaluation follows one deterministic execution pipeline.

Every evaluation executes the same sequence of business steps.

The pipeline is immutable from a business perspective.

---

## Evaluation Pipeline

```
RiskEvaluationRequest

↓

Request Validation

↓

EffectiveRiskRuleSet Resolution

↓

RiskEvaluationContext Construction

↓

Immutable Snapshots

↓

Observed Metrics

↓

Projection Engine

↓

Projected Metrics

↓

Derived Metrics

↓

Applicable Rule Selection

↓

Rule Evaluation

↓

Result Aggregation

↓

RiskValidationResult
```

Each stage produces immutable output for the following stage.

---

## Complete Evaluation

The engine evaluates every applicable rule.

Business evaluation never stops after the first blocking violation.

Although the final decision may already be known, complete evaluation provides:

- better explainability;
- complete audit information;
- improved analytics;
- better educational feedback.

---

## Evaluation Status

Business decision and evaluation execution are independent concepts.

Execution may end with:

- COMPLETED
- INCOMPLETE
- FAILED

Business decisions exist only for completed evaluations.

Possible decisions remain:

- APPROVED
- APPROVED_WITH_WARNINGS
- REJECTED

---

## Fail Closed

If required information cannot be evaluated safely, authorization is denied.

Examples include:

- missing snapshots;
- inconsistent market data;
- unavailable metrics;
- failed mandatory rules.

The platform never assumes safety under uncertainty.

---

## Stable Evaluation Order

Rules are evaluated using a deterministic ordering.

Typical ordering includes:

- authority;
- category;
- priority;
- identifier.

Stable ordering improves:

- reproducibility;
- testing;
- reporting;
- debugging.

---

## Parallel Execution

Parallel execution is considered an implementation optimization.

It must never change:

- business results;
- ordering semantics;
- reproducibility;
- auditability.

The architecture therefore specifies logical ordering rather than physical execution.

# Consequences

The adopted architecture provides:

- deterministic evaluations;
- configurable business rules;
- immutable business context;
- reusable financial calculations;
- centralized projection logic;
- complete auditability;
- full traceability;
- explainable business decisions;
- infrastructure independence;
- support for future AI and analytics without compromising deterministic behavior.

The Risk Domain becomes one of the core business domains of the Trading OS and acts as the platform's capital protection layer.

---

# Alternatives Considered

Several alternative designs were rejected during architecture discussions.

## Boolean Validation

Returning a simple boolean value was rejected because it cannot explain or audit decisions.

---

## Embedded Financial Calculations

Embedding financial calculations directly inside rules was rejected because it duplicates business logic and creates inconsistencies.

---

## Rule Dependencies

Allowing rules to depend on one another was rejected because it increases coupling and makes evaluation difficult to understand and test.

---

## Generic Rule Composition

Generic logical operators such as OR or "3 of 5" were rejected.

The platform enforces mandatory compliance with applicable blocking rules rather than searching for acceptable compromises.

---

## Fail-Open Authorization

Authorizing trades when information is incomplete was rejected.

The platform follows a fail-closed philosophy.

---

# Conclusion

The Risk Engine is defined as a deterministic business engine responsible for protecting trading capital through reproducible, explainable and auditable risk evaluation.

Rather than implementing isolated validations, the platform introduces a complete Risk Domain composed of immutable business context, centralized financial metrics, deterministic projections, configurable rule policies and structured evaluation results.

This architecture provides a stable foundation for future platform evolution while preserving deterministic business behavior, supporting advanced analytics and enabling continuous user learning through trustworthy business information.

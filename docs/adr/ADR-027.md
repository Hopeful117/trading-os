# ADR-027 — Trade Planning Model

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Authors:** Ludovic Brot
- **Decision Type:** Core Domain Architecture
- **Related ADRs:**
    - ADR-023 — Market Intelligence Pipeline
    - ADR-024 — Observation Model
    - ADR-025 — AI Analysis Model
    - ADR-026 — Trading Opportunity Model

---

# 1. Context

Trading OS separates market understanding from trade execution.

Previous ADRs introduced the complete market intelligence pipeline:

- Market Artifacts
- Capabilities
- Capability Results
- Observations
- AI Analyses
- Trading Opportunities

At this point, the platform is capable of understanding and explaining market situations.

However, identifying an opportunity is not sufficient to execute a trade.

A trader still needs to determine:

- whether the opportunity should be traded;
- how the trade should be executed;
- how much capital should be allocated;
- where to place the stop loss;
- where to take profits;
- how the position should be managed after execution.

These decisions should not belong to TradingOpportunity.

A new business concept is therefore required.

---

# 2. Problem Statement

Trading Opportunities intentionally remain execution-independent.

They describe market situations.

They do not describe:

- entry strategy;
- stop loss;
- take profit;
- position sizing;
- execution timing;
- position management.

Likewise, broker orders should not contain strategic information.

Broker orders simply represent execution instructions.

Trading OS therefore requires an intermediate business object capable of transforming market intelligence into an executable trading strategy while remaining independent from both broker implementation details and execution.

---

# 3. Decision

Introduce a new aggregate root named **TradePlan**.

TradePlan represents a complete execution strategy derived from one or more Trading Opportunities and adapted to a specific Trading Context.

TradePlan is not:

- a Trading Opportunity;
- a broker order;
- an open position;
- an executed trade.

Instead, it represents the complete strategy that could eventually be executed if validated.

---

# 4. Architectural Position

Trade Planning introduces a dedicated planning layer between market intelligence and execution.

```text
                 Trading Opportunity
                         │
                         ▼
               Trade Planning Engine
                         │
                         ▼
                     Trade Plan
                         │
                         ▼
                   Risk Validation
                         │
                         ▼
                  Execution Engine
                         │
                         ▼
                    Broker Service
                         │
                         ▼
                  Executed Trade
```

Each layer owns a single responsibility.

Trading Opportunities explain the market.

Trade Plans explain how to trade it.

Risk Validation determines whether the strategy is authorized.

Execution performs the strategy.

---

# 5. Decision 1 — Trade Plan Nature

## Decision

TradePlan represents a complete execution proposal.

It describes how a Trading Opportunity may be exploited.

It does not represent an execution.

---

## Motivation

A trader may prepare multiple execution strategies before deciding to trade.

Likewise, Trading OS should be capable of proposing strategies without forcing execution.

Examples include:

- reviewing opportunities;
- preparing orders before market open;
- paper trading;
- simulations;
- post-trade reviews.

Separating planning from execution allows these workflows without introducing broker dependencies.

---

## Consequences

TradePlan becomes a first-class business object.

It may:

- be created;
- be reviewed;
- be modified through versioning;
- be rejected;
- expire;
- be executed later;
- never be executed.

Execution is therefore only one possible outcome.

---

# 6. Decision 2 — Relationship with Trading Opportunities

## Decision

A TradePlan is derived from one or more Trading Opportunities.

Trading Opportunities remain reusable.

Trade Plans remain context-specific.

---

## Motivation

Most Trade Plans will originate from a single Opportunity.

Example:

```text
Bullish Breakout Opportunity
              │
              ▼
        Long Trade Plan
```

However, more advanced strategies may combine several Opportunities.

Example:

```text
Breakout Opportunity
          │
          │
Liquidity Opportunity
          │
          │
Macro Opportunity
          ▼
      Trade Plan
```

This architecture supports future evolution without modifying the domain model.

---

## Relationship

```text
Trading Opportunity

        │

      1..*

        ▼

Trade Plan
```

A TradePlan maintains explicit references to every Opportunity contributing to its creation.

---

## Consequences

The platform can fully explain:

- why a Trade Plan exists;
- which Opportunities contributed;
- which analyses supported each Opportunity;
- how market intelligence evolved into an executable strategy.

---

# 7. Decision 3 — Immutability and Versioning

## Decision

Trade Plans are immutable.

Any significant modification creates a new version.

A newer version never silently replaces an existing one.

Instead, it becomes a new execution proposal.

---

## Motivation

Trading strategies evolve.

Market conditions evolve.

Trader decisions evolve.

Replacing an existing Trade Plan would destroy valuable information.

Versioning preserves:

- historical decisions;
- trader behaviour;
- AI recommendations;
- execution rationale;
- post-trade analysis.

---

## Versioning

```text
Trade Plan V1
        │
        ▼
Trade Plan V2
        │
        ▼
Trade Plan V3
```

Every version remains immutable.

---

## Open Position Behaviour

If a position is already open:

Trade Plan V2 does not automatically replace V1.

Instead:

```text
Trade Plan V1

↓

Position Open

↓

Market Changed

↓

Trade Plan V2 (Proposed)

↓

Trader Decision
```

Possible decisions include:

- accept;
- ignore;
- postpone;
- close the position.

Future automated modes may delegate this decision to an authorized decision engine.

---

## Consequences

Planning history remains complete.

Every strategy proposed by the platform remains reproducible.

Every trader decision remains auditable.

---

# 8. Decision 4 — Internal Structure

## Decision

TradePlan separates execution parameters from trading rationale.

These represent two distinct responsibilities.

---

## Execution Parameters

Examples include:

- instrument;
- direction;
- entry strategy;
- entry price;
- stop loss;
- take profit;
- position sizing;
- expected risk/reward;
- expiration.

---

## Trading Rationale

Examples include:

- originating Opportunities;
- supporting Observations;
- AI Analyses;
- market thesis;
- confirmation conditions;
- invalidation conditions;
- explanations.

---

## Structure

```text
TradePlan

│

├── Execution Parameters

│      ├── Entry

│      ├── Stop Loss

│      ├── Take Profit

│      ├── Position Size

│      └── Expiration

│

└── Trading Rationale

       ├── Opportunities

       ├── Observations

       ├── AI Analyses

       ├── Market Thesis

       ├── Confirmation Rules

       └── Invalidation Rules
```

---

## Consequences

Execution logic remains independent from market reasoning.

Risk Validation focuses primarily on execution parameters.

User interfaces may display both the execution strategy and the reasoning behind it.

Future AI learning may compare:

- proposed reasoning;
- executed strategy;
- final outcome.

# 9. Decision 5 — Trade Plan Lifecycle

## Decision

A TradePlan owns its own lifecycle.

Its lifecycle is independent from:

- Trading Opportunities
- Risk Validation
- Broker Orders
- Positions
- Executed Trades

Each TradePlan evolves according to business decisions and market evolution.

---

## Lifecycle

```text
               DRAFT
                  │
                  ▼
              PROPOSED
                  │
       ┌──────────┼──────────┐
       ▼          ▼          ▼
 ACCEPTED    REJECTED    EXPIRED
       │
       ▼
RISK_VALIDATED
       │
       ▼
READY_TO_EXECUTE
       │
       ▼
EXECUTED
```

---

## State Definitions

### DRAFT

The Trade Plan is still being assembled.

Planning policies may continue enriching the strategy.

The plan is not yet visible as a complete proposal.

---

### PROPOSED

The Trade Plan is complete.

It is presented as a recommendation to the trader.

No business validation has yet been performed.

---

### ACCEPTED

The trader accepts the proposed strategy.

Acceptance does not imply authorization.

The strategy must still satisfy every risk rule.

---

### REJECTED

The trader explicitly refuses the proposed strategy.

Rejected Trade Plans remain available for:

- analytics
- audits
- AI learning
- trading journal

---

### EXPIRED

The strategy is no longer considered valid.

Typical causes include:

- market evolution
- Opportunity invalidation
- expiration time reached
- Trading Context modification

Expired plans remain historically available.

---

### RISK_VALIDATED

The Risk Engine confirms that this exact Trade Plan version satisfies every applicable rule.

Validation applies only to the validated version.

---

### READY_TO_EXECUTE

The strategy is fully approved.

Execution may still wait for external conditions.

Examples:

- price reaching the entry zone
- market opening
- confirmation candle
- trader confirmation

---

### EXECUTED

The Trade Plan has been used to create one or more broker orders.

Execution history continues through Position and Trade aggregates.

The Trade Plan itself remains immutable.

---

## Motivation

Planning, validation and execution represent different business responsibilities.

Keeping separate lifecycle states improves:

- explainability
- auditing
- traceability
- future automation

---

# 10. Decision 6 — Trade Planning Engine

## Decision

Trade Plans may only be created by the Trade Planning Engine.

The Trade Planning Engine becomes the unique orchestration entry point.

---

## Architecture

```text
Trading Opportunities
          │
          ▼
Trade Planning Engine
          │
 ┌────────┴────────┐
 ▼                 ▼
Planning Policies  AI Planning
          │
          ▼
TradePlan Builder
          │
          ▼
Trade Plan
```

---

## Responsibilities

The Trade Planning Engine:

- retrieves Trading Opportunities
- retrieves Trading Context
- invokes planning policies
- integrates optional AI planning
- validates planning prerequisites
- determines whether a new version is required
- invokes the TradePlan Builder

---

## Non-responsibilities

The Trade Planning Engine never:

- detects Opportunities
- computes indicators
- validates risk
- executes broker orders
- manages open positions
- modifies existing Trade Plans

---

## Motivation

Planning is orchestration.

Planning logic remains delegated to dedicated policies.

This preserves separation of concerns and future extensibility.

---

# 11. Decision 7 — Trading Context

## Decision

Trade Plans are not global market objects.

They are created for a specific Trading Context.

---

## Motivation

Execution depends on trader-specific information.

Examples include:

- account size
- available capital
- risk profile
- leverage
- prop firm constraints
- existing exposure
- account currency
- trading preferences

Two traders observing the same Opportunity may legitimately receive different Trade Plans.

---

## Example

```text
Trading Opportunity

        │

 ┌──────┴─────────┐

 ▼                ▼

FTMO Account   Personal Account

 ▼                ▼

Trade Plan A   Trade Plan B
```

---

## Trading Context

Trading Context may include:

- trading account
- portfolio
- risk profile
- prop firm profile
- execution preferences
- active exposure
- available buying power

Future extensions remain possible without modifying TradePlan itself.

---

## Consequences

Trade Planning becomes personalized.

Trading Opportunities remain globally reusable.

Trade Plans become account-specific execution strategies.

---

# 12. Decision 8 — Continuous Replanning

## Decision

Trade Plans are never modified automatically.

Whenever significant market changes occur, Trading OS may generate a new Trade Plan version.

The existing version remains unchanged.

---

## Replanning Flow

```text
Trade Plan V1

        │

Market Changes

        │

        ▼

Trade Plan V2

        │

        ▼

Trader Decision
```

---

## Possible Triggers

Examples include:

- Opportunity evolution
- new AI interpretation
- volatility changes
- macroeconomic events
- invalidated assumptions
- Trading Context evolution
- user preference changes

---

## Trader Decisions

The trader may:

- accept the new proposal
- ignore it
- postpone the decision
- close the position
- request another analysis

Future automation may replace the trader with an authorized decision engine.

---

## Motivation

Trading OS should remain proactive.

It should never silently modify a trading strategy.

Recommendations evolve.

Execution decisions remain explicit.

---

## Consequences

Planning history remains complete.

Every recommendation becomes auditable.

Future analytics can compare:

- generated plans
- accepted plans
- rejected plans
- ignored plans
- execution performance
- trader behaviour

# 13. Decision 9 — Risk Validation Snapshot

## Decision

Once a TradePlan has been validated by the Risk Engine and reaches the
`READY_TO_EXECUTE` state, that specific version becomes an immutable execution
snapshot.

Any subsequent modification requires the creation of a new TradePlan version
and a new Risk Validation cycle.

---

## Motivation

Risk validation certifies a precise execution strategy.

It does not certify an abstract TradePlan concept.

If execution parameters change after validation, the previous validation is no
longer guaranteed to remain valid.

Typical examples include:

- modified stop loss;
- modified take profit;
- modified position sizing;
- modified entry strategy;
- modified execution timing.

Each of these changes may alter the calculated risk.

---

## Validation Model

```text
Trade Plan V1
      │
      ▼
Risk Validation
      │
      ▼
READY_TO_EXECUTE
```

If the strategy changes:

```text
Trade Plan V1
      │
      ▼
READY_TO_EXECUTE
      │
Market Changes
      │
      ▼
Trade Plan V2
      │
      ▼
Risk Validation
      │
      ▼
READY_TO_EXECUTE
```

Every version owns its own validation.

---

## Consequences

The Risk Engine always validates one immutable snapshot.

Execution always references the exact validated version.

Historical validation remains reproducible.

Auditing becomes significantly simpler.

---

# 14. Decision 10 — TradePlan as a Complete Execution Strategy

## Decision

A TradePlan represents a complete execution strategy.

It is not limited to an entry price, stop loss and take profit.

---

## Motivation

Professional trading involves considerably more than placing an order.

A complete strategy may include:

- entry conditions;
- initial risk;
- exit targets;
- position management;
- invalidation conditions;
- expiration rules;
- monitoring recommendations.

TradePlan therefore represents the entire strategic intention.

---

## Strategy Structure

```text
Trade Plan

│

├── Entry Strategy

├── Initial Risk

├── Exit Strategy

├── Position Management Strategy

├── Invalidation Rules

├── Expiration Rules

└── Trading Thesis
```

---

## Position Management

Examples of management rules include:

- move stop loss to break-even;
- partial profit taking;
- trailing stop recommendation;
- volatility-based adaptation;
- early exit recommendation;
- session-based exit.

TradePlan defines these rules.

Future execution components decide whether and how they are applied.

---

## Responsibilities

TradePlan specifies strategy.

Execution Engine executes strategy.

Position Manager supervises strategy.

Broker Service communicates with the broker.

Each component remains independent.

---

## Consequences

TradePlan becomes a reusable strategic artifact.

It supports:

- discretionary trading;
- paper trading;
- backtesting;
- simulations;
- AI evaluation;
- post-trade analysis;
- future execution automation.

---

# 15. Architectural Consequences

This ADR introduces a dedicated planning layer inside Trading OS.

The resulting architecture becomes:

```text
Market Intelligence
        │
        ▼
Trading Opportunity
        │
        ▼
Trade Planning
        │
        ▼
Trade Plan
        │
        ▼
Risk Validation
        │
        ▼
Execution
        │
        ▼
Trade
```

The planning layer remains completely independent from:

- market intelligence;
- broker implementations;
- execution mechanisms.

# 16. Acceptance Criteria

This ADR is considered implemented when:

- TradePlan aggregate exists.
- TradePlan is immutable.
- Versioning is implemented.
- Lifecycle is implemented.
- Trade Planning Engine is the only creation entry point.
- Trade Planning policies exist.
- Trade Plans are generated from Trading Opportunities.
- Trade Plans require a Trading Context.
- Continuous replanning generates new versions.
- Risk validation always validates immutable snapshots.
- TradePlan models a complete execution strategy.
- Unit and integration tests validate every business rule.

---

# 17. Definition of Done

The implementation is complete when:

- every architectural decision has been implemented;
- TradePlan remains immutable;
- planning is orchestrated exclusively by the Trade Planning Engine;
- Trading Context is required during planning;
- version history is preserved;
- Risk Validation always references a specific immutable version;
- execution never modifies an existing TradePlan;
- all tests pass successfully;
- the implementation complies with the related ADRs.

# ADR-029 – Execution Domain Architecture

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-07-31

**Authors:** Ludovic Brot

---

# Executive Summary

This ADR defines the architecture of the Execution Domain within the Trading Core.

The Execution Domain is responsible for transforming an approved trading decision into a deterministic broker execution while guaranteeing:

- deterministic execution behavior
- complete auditability
- broker independence
- idempotent order submission
- explicit lifecycle management
- failure recovery
- execution reconciliation

The Execution Domain does not perform market analysis, strategy selection or risk evaluation.
Its responsibility begins only after the Risk Domain has approved a TradePlan.

---

# Context

The Trading Core is organized around explicit business domains.

Market analysis produces trading opportunities.

Trading planning transforms those opportunities into executable TradePlans.

The Risk Domain evaluates every TradePlan and decides whether execution is allowed.

Only approved TradePlans may enter the Execution Domain.

Execution introduces several technical and business challenges:

- duplicate submissions
- broker communication failures
- partial executions
- retries
- reconciliation
- asynchronous broker events
- audit requirements
- multiple broker support

Without a dedicated execution model these concerns rapidly become mixed together inside services, making the system difficult to maintain and unsafe.

---

# Problem Statement

Executing a trade is significantly more complex than simply calling a broker API.

The system must guarantee that:

- one approved TradePlan produces at most one logical execution
- retries never create duplicate orders
- failures can be recovered safely
- broker-specific behavior remains isolated
- execution history remains fully auditable
- execution lifecycle is explicit
- recovery decisions are deterministic

These requirements justify introducing a dedicated Execution Domain.

---

# Decision

The Trading Core shall contain an explicit Execution Domain responsible for transforming approved TradePlans into broker executions.

The Execution Domain owns:

- execution orchestration
- execution lifecycle
- idempotency
- broker communication
- execution recovery
- execution reconciliation
- execution audit

The Execution Domain never performs:

- market analysis
- strategy selection
- risk evaluation
- portfolio optimization

Those responsibilities belong to other domains.

---

# Architectural Principles

The Execution Domain follows these principles.

## Business First

Execution models business concepts rather than broker APIs.

---

## Broker Independence

The domain never depends directly on Kraken or any other broker.

Broker-specific behavior is isolated behind ports and adapters.

---

## Deterministic Execution

Every execution decision must be reproducible from the domain state.

---

## Explicit Lifecycle

Execution progresses through explicit lifecycle states.

No hidden transitions are allowed.

---

## Idempotency by Design

Duplicate execution requests shall never create duplicate logical orders.

---

## Recovery Before Retry

Whenever execution outcome is uncertain the system performs reconciliation before retrying.

---

## Immutable Audit History

Every meaningful execution event produces an immutable domain event.

---

## Hexagonal Architecture

External systems remain outside the domain.

The Execution Domain communicates exclusively through ports.

---

## Single Responsibility

Execution is responsible only for execution.

Risk remains responsible for risk.

Broker Service remains responsible for broker connectivity.

---

# Domain Invariants

The following invariants must always hold.

## TradePlan

One ExecutionIntent references exactly one approved TradePlan.

---

## Approval

Execution may only start after Risk approval.

---

## Idempotency

Each ExecutionIntent owns exactly one immutable idempotency key.

---

## Active Attempt

Only one active ExecutionAttempt may exist at any given time.

---

## Terminal State

Completed, Cancelled or Expired ExecutionIntents cannot be executed again.

---

## Unknown State

Unknown submission results always require reconciliation before any retry.

---

## Audit

Every state transition produces immutable business events.

# Decision 2 – ExecutionIntent

ExecutionIntent is introduced as a persistent aggregate.

ExecutionIntent represents the business authorization to execute a TradePlan.

It forms the security boundary between Risk approval and broker submission.

ExecutionIntent owns:

- identifier
- TradePlan reference
- Risk approval reference
- lifecycle state
- timestamps
- execution metadata
- version
- idempotency key
- initiator
- expiration

ExecutionIntent is persisted through the ExecutionIntentRepositoryPort.

---

# Decision 3 – Lifecycle Separation

Execution lifecycle is separated into independent concepts.

TradePlan

↓

ExecutionIntent

↓

ExecutionAttempt(s)

↓

BrokerOrder

↓

Broker Events

Each lifecycle evolves independently.

One ExecutionIntent may own multiple ExecutionAttempts without creating multiple logical executions.

This separation simplifies retries, reconciliation and audit while preserving a single business execution.

# Decision 4 – Idempotency & Concurrency

Execution reliability depends on deterministic idempotency.

Idempotency is a business responsibility of the Execution Domain rather than a transport-level concern.

HTTP retries, broker retries or infrastructure retries must never be trusted to guarantee business correctness.

## ExecutionIntent

ExecutionIntent owns a stable, immutable idempotency key.

This key uniquely identifies the logical execution of a TradePlan.

It never changes during the lifetime of the ExecutionIntent.

## ExecutionAttempt

Each submission attempt owns its own identifier.

An ExecutionAttempt contains:

- executionAttemptId
- attemptNumber
- creation timestamp
- execution status
- broker submission metadata
- broker correlation identifiers
- execution result

ExecutionAttempt identifiers are unique for every submission while sharing the same ExecutionIntent.

## Concurrency

ExecutionIntent uses optimistic locking.

The domain guarantees:

- one active submission at a time
- immutable idempotency key
- no duplicate logical executions
- deterministic conflict resolution

Concurrent execution requests attempting to submit the same ExecutionIntent shall fail deterministically.

## Unknown Submission State

The most critical execution state is an unknown broker outcome.

Possible causes include:

- network timeout
- application crash
- broker timeout
- lost acknowledgement

In this situation the system cannot determine whether the broker accepted the order.

The Execution Domain enters:

SUBMISSION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN

No retry is allowed until reconciliation has completed.

# Decision 5 – Execution Pipeline

Execution is implemented as an explicit processing pipeline.

Each stage owns one responsibility.

```
Execution Validation
        │
        ▼
Idempotency Verification
        │
        ▼
ExecutionAttempt Creation
        │
        ▼
Broker Submission
        │
        ▼
Broker Response Processing
        │
        ▼
Execution Finalization
```

## Execution Validation

Validates:

- Risk approval
- ExecutionIntent state
- expiration
- business invariants

## Idempotency Verification

Ensures:

- no duplicate execution
- immutable execution identity
- concurrency safety

## ExecutionAttempt Creation

Creates a new attempt representing one broker submission.

No communication with the broker occurs before the attempt exists.

## Broker Submission

The Broker Service receives an ExecutionRequest containing:

- executionIntentId
- executionAttemptId
- idempotencyKey
- execution parameters

The Broker Service remains unaware of TradePlans or Risk.

## Response Processing

Broker responses are translated into domain concepts.

Broker-specific payloads never enter the domain model.

## Finalization

The Execution Domain updates:

- ExecutionIntent
- ExecutionAttempt
- BrokerOrder

and emits the appropriate domain events.

# Decision 6 – Domain Events & Audit

The Execution Domain maintains immutable business history through domain events.

Current aggregate state answers:

"What is the current situation?"

Domain events answer:

"How did we reach this situation?"

This ADR intentionally does not adopt Event Sourcing.

Aggregates remain the source of truth.

Events provide immutable audit history.

# Decision 7 – Recovery & Reconciliation

Recovery is an execution capability.

It is not a separate business aggregate.

Recovery is implemented as an explicit Recovery Pipeline.

## Recovery Responsibilities

Recovery is responsible for:

- locating unfinished executions
- inspecting current execution state
- determining recovery strategy
- reconciling broker state
- updating aggregates
- emitting recovery events
- safely resuming execution
- blocking unsafe execution

## Recovery Pipeline

```
Recovery Startup
        │
        ▼
Find Recoverable ExecutionIntents
        │
        ▼
Inspect Current State
        │
        ▼
Determine Recovery Strategy
        │
        ▼
Broker Reconciliation
        │
        ▼
Update Domain State
        │
        ▼
Emit Recovery Events
        │
        ▼
Resume / Complete / Block
```

## Recoverable States

Recovery applies only to non-terminal states.

Typical recoverable states include:

- SUBMISSION_IN_PROGRESS
- SUBMISSION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN
- RECONCILIATION_IN_PROGRESS

Terminal states are excluded:

- COMPLETED
- CANCELLED
- EXPIRED

## Recovery Principle

Recovery never assumes an order was not created.

Whenever uncertainty exists:

1. inspect current state
2. reconcile with broker
3. update aggregates
4. decide whether execution may continue

Retry is never the first action.

# Security Considerations

Execution represents the boundary between business approval and financial action.

Therefore:

- Risk approval is mandatory
- ExecutionIntent is immutable after approval
- Idempotency is mandatory
- Recovery is deterministic
- Every transition is auditable
- Broker communication is isolated
- Domain invariants cannot be bypassed

These principles reduce operational and financial risk.

# Consequences

## Benefits

- deterministic execution
- explicit business model
- broker independence
- safe retries
- complete audit history
- simplified testing
- high maintainability
- clear separation of concerns
- future multi-broker support
- scalable architecture

## Costs

The architecture introduces additional domain objects and lifecycle management.

This additional complexity is intentional and reflects the inherent complexity of financial order execution.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Direct Broker Calls

Rejected.

This approach mixes execution logic with infrastructure and makes idempotency and recovery difficult.

## Single Execution Entity

Rejected.

ExecutionIntent, ExecutionAttempt and BrokerOrder represent different business concepts with independent lifecycles.

Combining them would increase coupling and reduce clarity.

## Recovery Aggregate

Rejected.

Recovery currently represents a domain capability rather than an independent business identity.

A dedicated Recovery aggregate may be introduced in the future if operational complexity significantly increases.

---

# Conclusion

The Execution Domain provides a deterministic, auditable and broker-independent execution architecture.

Together with ADR-028 (Risk Engine Architecture), this ADR establishes the transactional foundation of the Trading Core.

The Risk Domain determines whether a trade may be executed.

The Execution Domain guarantees that approved decisions are executed safely, consistently and recoverably.

# ADR-002 — Microservice Architecture

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-23

## Context

Trading OS combines multiple technical domains that evolve independently:

- user management;
- broker connectivity;
- market data;
- news and macroeconomic information;
- AI analysis;
- trading logic;
- frontend.

Building all responsibilities inside a single application would create a tightly coupled architecture, making future evolution difficult and increasing deployment risks.

The platform is expected to continuously evolve over several years while integrating additional brokers, market data providers and AI capabilities.

## Decision

Trading OS adopts a microservice architecture.

Each service owns a clearly identified business responsibility and communicates with the others through HTTP APIs.

The initial architecture consists of the following services.

### Gateway

Responsibilities:

- single entry point for frontend clients;
- JWT validation;
- request routing;
- cross-cutting concerns.

The Gateway should not contain business logic.

---

### Trading Core

Responsibilities:

- business rules;
- Risk Engine;
- account management;
- trade management;
- rule validation;
- orchestration of external services.

Trading Core is considered the central business service of the platform.

---

### Broker Service

Responsibilities:

- broker abstraction;
- account synchronization;
- order execution;
- broker-specific adapters.

The Broker Service isolates Trading Core from broker APIs.

---

### Market Data Service

Responsibilities:

- market catalog;
- market constraints;
- market states;
- realtime market streams;
- market synchronization.

This service owns all market-related reference data.

---

### News Service

Responsibilities:

- economic calendar;
- macroeconomic events;
- financial news;
- market-impact metadata.

This service provides contextual information used by both deterministic services and AI.

---

### AI Engine

Responsibilities:

- market interpretation;
- opportunity ranking;
- recommendation generation;
- historical learning;
- contextual reasoning.

The AI Engine never bypasses deterministic business rules.

---

### Frontend

Responsibilities:

- user interface;
- visualization;
- interaction;
- workflow support.

Business decisions remain server-side.

## Communication

Services communicate through HTTP APIs.

The Gateway is the only public entry point exposed to frontend applications.

Internal business communication is performed between services without exposing implementation details to users.

## Ownership

Each service owns its own business domain.

Examples:

Broker Service owns broker integration.

Market Data Service owns market reference data.

Trading Core owns business logic.

The AI Engine owns interpretation and recommendation generation.

No service should directly manipulate another service's persistence layer.

## Consequences

Advantages:

- independent deployment;
- clear business boundaries;
- easier testing;
- scalability;
- easier addition of brokers or providers.

Trade-offs:

- increased operational complexity;
- inter-service communication;
- distributed debugging.

These trade-offs are considered acceptable given the long-term objectives of Trading OS.

## Alternatives Considered

### Modular Monolith

Rejected.

Although simpler initially, it would progressively mix unrelated responsibilities and reduce long-term flexibility.

### AI-Centric Monolith

Rejected.

AI should remain an important service but should not become responsible for deterministic business logic.

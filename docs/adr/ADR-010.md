# ADR-010 — Market State Domain Model

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-23

## Context

Market availability is a fundamental concept throughout Trading OS.

Multiple platform components depend on knowing whether a market can currently be traded:

- Trading Core
- Risk Engine
- Market Data Service
- Active Scanner
- Passive Scanner
- AI Engine
- Frontend

Initially, market availability was represented by a simple `MarketStatus` enumeration stored directly inside the `Market` entity.

As the platform evolved, it became clear that market availability represents a business concept with its own lifecycle and should therefore become an independent value object.

## Decision

Trading OS replaces the previous `MarketStatus` field with an embedded `MarketState` value object.

The `MarketState` object represents the current tradability of a market.

It is intentionally separated from static market metadata.

---

## Definition of OPEN

Within Trading OS, a market is considered **OPEN** only when an order can be executed immediately.

Examples of OPEN include:

- continuous Forex trading sessions;
- actively traded cryptocurrencies;
- exchange sessions where orders are executable.

Examples considered CLOSED include:

- pre-market sessions;
- post-market sessions;
- exchange maintenance;
- suspended markets;
- broker maintenance periods.

The objective is to model tradability rather than exchange schedules.

---

## Responsibilities

`MarketState` is responsible for representing the current execution state of a market.

It does not contain:

- market constraints;
- technical analysis;
- business rules;
- trading decisions.

Its purpose is limited to describing whether trading is currently possible.

---

## Ownership

`MarketState` belongs to the Market Data Service.

Trading Core consumes this information but never owns or modifies it.

Updates originate from market providers.

---

## Business Usage

Trading Core uses `MarketState` to determine whether trading may proceed.

Typical validations include:

- preventing order submission on closed markets;
- validating user-defined trading rules;
- preventing execution during maintenance periods.

AI may use MarketState as contextual information but never modify it.

---

## Synchronization

MarketState is refreshed during market synchronization.

Realtime updates may also modify MarketState when supported by market providers.

---

## Design Principles

MarketState should remain:

- deterministic;
- provider-independent;
- lightweight;
- easily reusable.

Business logic consuming MarketState should not require knowledge of provider-specific APIs.

---

## Consequences

Advantages:

- clearer domain model;
- improved readability;
- reusable business object;
- easier future extensions;
- separation between static and dynamic market information.

Trade-offs:

- additional embedded object;
- migration from the previous `MarketStatus` field.

These trade-offs are accepted.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Keep MarketStatus Inside Market

Rejected.

Market availability has its own business meaning and deserves a dedicated value object.

---

### Model Exchange Sessions Directly

Rejected.

Exchange sessions do not necessarily indicate whether an order can actually be executed.

Trading OS models tradability rather than exchange operating hours.

Exchange session information may later become part of the News Service or another contextual domain.

# ADR-005 — Trading Core Responsibilities

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-23

## Context

Trading OS is composed of multiple specialized services.

As the platform evolved, it became necessary to clearly define the responsibilities of the Trading Core in order to prevent it from becoming a generic gateway or a simple proxy between services.

The Trading Core represents the business heart of the platform.

It is responsible for enforcing trading rules, validating business decisions and orchestrating the various specialized services.

The Trading Core does **not** own every business domain.

Instead, each specialized service remains responsible for its own data and behaviour.

## Decision

Trading Core is the central business orchestration service of Trading OS.

Its responsibilities are limited to business logic and decision making.

It consumes information produced by specialized services but does not duplicate their responsibilities.

---

## Responsibilities

Trading Core owns:

- account management;
- trade management;
- position management;
- Risk Engine;
- trading rule enforcement;
- user-defined trading profiles;
- business workflows;
- dashboard aggregation;
- orchestration of business services.

Trading Core is responsible for deciding whether a trade may be executed.

---

## Service Consumption

Trading Core may consume information from external services through dedicated clients.

Examples include:

### Broker Service

Used for:

- accounts;
- balances;
- positions;
- order execution.

---

### Market Data Service

Used for:

- market constraints;
- market state;
- market metadata;
- reference market information.

Trading Core consumes this information to validate trades but does not expose it again without adding business value.

---

### News Service

Used for:

- economic calendar;
- macroeconomic events;
- news metadata.

These data are consumed by deterministic rules and AI.

---

### AI Engine

Used for:

- market recommendations;
- opportunity rankings;
- contextual explanations.

Trading Core may consume AI outputs but remains responsible for final business validation.

---

## Business Ownership

Trading Core owns business decisions.

Examples include:

- validating whether trading is currently allowed;
- enforcing trading profiles;
- validating risk limits;
- determining whether market conditions satisfy business constraints.

Trading Core never delegates deterministic decisions to AI.

---

## API Responsibilities

Trading Core exposes APIs only when it owns or enriches the underlying information.

Examples:

- Dashboard
- Trades
- Positions
- Trading statistics

Trading Core should not expose pass-through APIs that merely forward another service's data without additional business value.

Reference data remain exposed by their owning services.

---

## Persistence

Trading Core stores only business entities that belong to its own domain.

Examples include:

- User
- Account
- Trade
- TradingProfile
- RiskRules

It must never persist another service's internal entities.

---

## Consequences

Advantages:

- clear business ownership;
- simpler service boundaries;
- improved maintainability;
- reduced duplication;
- scalable architecture.

Trade-offs:

- additional inter-service communication;
- orchestration logic concentrated inside Trading Core.

These trade-offs are accepted because Trading Core is intentionally designed as the business brain of Trading OS.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Trading Core as API Proxy

Rejected.

Simply forwarding requests between the frontend and specialized services provides little business value and unnecessarily couples services.

---

### Fat Gateway

Rejected.

Business logic belongs inside Trading Core, not inside Gateway.

Gateway remains responsible only for technical concerns.

---

### Shared Business Logic Across Services

Rejected.

Business rules should remain centralized to ensure consistent behaviour.

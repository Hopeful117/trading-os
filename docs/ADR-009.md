# ADR-009 — Trading Rules and Risk Profiles

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-23

## Context

Trading platforms are often designed around a specific trading style or proprietary trading firm's rules.

Trading OS has a different objective.

The platform must remain useful for:

- personal trading;
- proprietary trading challenges;
- professional discretionary trading;
- future custom workflows.

Different users may require different trading constraints while sharing the same trading infrastructure.

Examples include:

- FTMO challenge rules;
- The5ers challenge rules;
- personal money management rules;
- conservative trading profiles;
- aggressive trading profiles.

Embedding these rules directly into Trading Core would unnecessarily couple business logic with specific providers or trading methodologies.

## Decision

Trading OS introduces configurable Trading Rule Profiles.

A Trading Rule Profile defines a complete set of deterministic trading constraints.

The Risk Engine validates every trading operation against the currently active profile.

Trading OS never hardcodes proprietary trading firm rules.

Challenge providers become predefined configurations rather than business logic.

---

## Responsibilities

A Trading Rule Profile may define:

- maximum risk per trade;
- maximum daily loss;
- maximum account drawdown;
- maximum simultaneous positions;
- minimum risk/reward ratio;
- allowed markets;
- forbidden trading periods;
- overnight restrictions;
- weekend restrictions;
- custom user-defined rules.

Profiles may evolve independently from the Risk Engine.

---

## Risk Engine

The Risk Engine remains responsible for deterministic validation.

Before every trade, it evaluates:

- account status;
- market state;
- trading profile;
- market constraints;
- current exposure;
- deterministic business rules.

Only valid trades may proceed further.

---

## Challenge Compatibility

Proprietary trading challenges are supported by providing predefined Trading Rule Profiles.

Examples include:

- FTMO Evaluation
- The5ers
- Personal Trading
- Swing Trading
- Scalping
- Crypto Trading

Trading OS itself does not contain provider-specific business logic.

---

## User Customization

Users may create their own profiles.

Examples:

- Maximum 0.5% risk per trade.
- Never trade during high-impact economic events.
- Only trade Forex.
- Maximum two simultaneous positions.

This allows Trading OS to support virtually any trading methodology.

---

## AI Integration

Artificial Intelligence consumes Trading Rule Profiles but never modifies them.

Recommendations generated by AI remain subject to deterministic validation.

Example workflow:

```
AI Recommendation
        │
        ▼
Trading Rule Profile
        │
        ▼
Risk Engine
        │
        ▼
User Decision
```

The AI Engine may explain violations but cannot bypass them.

---

## Design Principles

Trading Rule Profiles remain:

- deterministic;
- versionable;
- explainable;
- configurable.

Business behaviour should always be reproducible.

---

## Consequences

Advantages:

- provider independence;
- reusable business logic;
- configurable trading behaviour;
- easier testing;
- long-term flexibility.

Trade-offs:

- increased configuration complexity;
- profile management lifecycle.

These trade-offs are accepted.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Hardcoded FTMO Rules

Rejected.

Trading OS should remain a general trading platform.

Proprietary trading firms represent only one use case.

---

### AI Determines Risk Rules

Rejected.

Risk management must remain deterministic.

AI may provide recommendations but never modify business constraints.

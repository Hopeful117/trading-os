# ADR-011 — Dynamic Market Subscription Strategy

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-23

## Context

Trading OS consumes realtime market data from external providers using WebSocket connections.

During the initial implementation, the Market Data Service established a permanent connection and subscribed to a fixed set of markets for development purposes.

While suitable for validation and testing, this approach does not scale to production.

Maintaining subscriptions for every available market would:

- consume unnecessary network bandwidth;
- increase infrastructure costs;
- waste computational resources;
- process market data that may never be used.

Trading OS therefore requires a subscription strategy driven by actual business needs.

---

## Decision

Trading OS adopts a dynamic subscription strategy.

Realtime subscriptions are created and released according to the needs of platform components.

The Market Data Service becomes responsible for subscription lifecycle management.

Subscriptions are considered temporary resources rather than permanent infrastructure.

---

## Subscription Sources

Realtime subscriptions may be requested by:

- Passive Scanner;
- Active Scanner;
- Position Monitoring Agent;
- frontend market detail pages;
- future analytics services.

Each consumer subscribes only to the markets required for its current task.

---

## Passive Scanner

The Passive Scanner maintains a limited set of subscriptions according to its configured scope.

Typical examples include:

- user watchlists;
- Forex markets;
- cryptocurrency markets;
- custom trading universes.

Its objective is to maintain market awareness while minimizing resource consumption.

---

## Active Scanner

The Active Scanner may temporarily expand the number of subscriptions while performing an on-demand market analysis.

Subscriptions should be released once the analysis is complete unless still required by another consumer.

---

## Position Monitoring Agent

The Position Monitoring Agent subscribes to markets associated with currently open positions.

Its objective is to detect situations requiring user attention.

Examples include:

- increased volatility;
- approaching high-impact news;
- significant price movements;
- deteriorating market conditions.

Subscriptions are automatically removed once positions are closed.

---

## User Interaction

Frontend applications may request temporary subscriptions.

Typical example:

```
Markets

↓

Select BTC/USD

↓

Subscribe BTC/USD

↓

Receive realtime MarketDataEvent

↓

Leave page

↓

Unsubscribe BTC/USD
```

Realtime data are therefore activated only when required.

---

## Shared Subscription Management

Multiple platform components may require the same market simultaneously.

The Market Data Service should manage subscriptions centrally.

Duplicate subscriptions should be avoided whenever possible.

Subscription lifecycle should be reference-counted or managed through an equivalent mechanism.

---

## Event Distribution

Provider-specific realtime messages are converted into provider-independent domain events.

Example:

```
Kraken WebSocket

↓

Provider DTO

↓

MarketDataEvent

↓

Consumers
```

Consumers remain independent from provider protocols.

---

## Design Principles

Subscription management should be:

- dynamic;
- provider-independent;
- reusable;
- resource-efficient;
- transparent to consumers.

Consumers request market streams without knowing provider-specific implementation details.

---

## Consequences

Advantages:

- reduced infrastructure costs;
- lower bandwidth consumption;
- scalable realtime architecture;
- simplified consumers;
- better separation of concerns.

Trade-offs:

- additional lifecycle management;
- subscription coordination;
- shared state management.

These trade-offs are accepted.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Permanent Global Subscriptions

Rejected.

Maintaining subscriptions for every market wastes infrastructure resources and does not scale.

---

### Consumer-Manages-Its-Own-WebSocket

Rejected.

Subscription lifecycle should remain centralized inside the Market Data Service.

This avoids duplicated connections and inconsistent provider interactions.
